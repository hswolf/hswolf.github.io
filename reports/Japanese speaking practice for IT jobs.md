# Rehearse the job, not the textbook

For a Vietnam-based engineer at N4–N3, the best way to build job-ready spoken Japanese is to rehearse aloud, with correction, the few routines that Japanese IT employers actually test: a one-minute self-introduction, a project explanation that holds up under follow-up questions, stand-up and delay reports, spec clarification with 復唱 (repeating back what was said), and an incident first report. The research supports a loop of brief planning, timed delivery, corrective feedback and spaced repetition of the same task. Corrective feedback produces medium, lasting gains across several meta-analyses; repeating a talk under shrinking time limits reliably speeds up speech, including among Vietnamese learners; and production practice beats comprehension practice on delayed tests of what learners can say. The stakes are concrete because the JLPT has no speaking section: employers filter CVs on N3 or N2, then judge speech in a Japanese interview. Japanese pays (postings in Vietnam requiring Japanese or Korean paid at least 38% above the market average in 2023), but mostly by opening Bridge SE roles that ask for N2, about two years of experience and proven client communication. Vietnamese speakers have a real head start, since about half of the two-kanji words in JLPT lists match Hán Việt words, but they carry predictable weaknesses in long vowels, mora rhythm, flat pitch and humble keigo, and native listeners weight prosody (pitch, length, rhythm) above individual sounds. Living in Vietnam is less of a handicap than it looks: community tutors cost about US$10–20 an hour, AI voice partners cost little or nothing, Japan is only two hours ahead, and N3-level developer and tester jobs at Japan-facing companies are the best substitute for immersion. Most of the research studies learners of English, so run the plan as an experiment and check it with monthly recordings of speech rate and pauses inside clauses.

## The JLPT certifies knowledge, but employers hire on speech

The JLPT tests language knowledge, reading and listening, and nothing else ([Wikipedia: JLPT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese-Language_Proficiency_Test)). Guides for Japanese employers warn that even N1 and N2 holders can struggle with keigo and business email. Companies therefore check Japanese in the interview itself, through the self-introduction and career explanation, job questions, role-plays, and keigo and manners ([TMG employer guide](https://www.hataraku.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/jinzai/kakuho/gaikokujinzaitohataraku_3.pdf); [TCJ](https://gaikoku-jinzai.tcj-education.com/posts/japanese_lang_skills3)). A Tokyo government guide adds that foreign candidates are easily judged on impression alone ([TIR Navi Center](https://tir-navicenter.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/assets/pdf/2026/2026_03.pdf)). The JLPT level on a CV gets a candidate into the room. Spoken Japanese decides what happens there.

The Vietnamese job market offers this learner a clear ladder. Offshore developer and tester roles at Japan-facing companies often accept **N3**, because a comtor (an IT interpreter) or a BrSE (Bridge SE, the engineer who links the Japanese client with the offshore team) handles the client ([CareerLink](https://www.careerlink.vn/cam-nang-viec-lam/?p=23679)). Recent examples include a Fujitsu Vietnam Software Engineer (Japanese N3) role in Hanoi and an FPT Software Manual Tester (Japanese N3) role ([VietnamWorks](https://www.vietnamworks.com/japanese-n3-kv); [VietnamWorks](https://www.vietnamworks.com/manual-tester-japanese-n3-1994200-jv)). Mainstream BrSE postings ask for **N2 plus about two years of experience**. FPT Software alone advertised 25 such openings in Hanoi in early 2026 ([bejob.vn](https://bejob.vn/viec-lam/fpt-software-brse-n2-up-p6953.html)), although a few, such as NTT DATA Vietnam's "BrSE (Japanese N2/N3)", accept N3 ([TopDev](https://topdev.vn/viec-lam/brse-japanese-n2-n3-ntt-data-vietnam-2029499)). Job fairs for Vietnamese candidates set a similar bar. The Tokyo Metropolitan Government's September 2026 fair for people in Vietnam asked for conversational-level Japanese or higher ([TMG job fair](https://tcg.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/person-vn)).

The money follows the role, not the certificate. TopDev found that postings requiring Japanese or Korean paid **at least 38% above the market average** in 2023 ([TopDev 2023](https://topdev.vn/TopDev_VietnamITMarketReport2023_VietnamTechTalentsReport.pdf)), and its 2024–2025 report puts BrSE gross pay at **US$1,370–2,400 a month** ([TopDev 2024–2025](https://shiftasia.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/TopDev_VietnamITMarketReport2024_2025_VietnamITTechTalentLandscape.pdf)). The certificate itself is worth little. One Japanese recruiter's BrSE listing pays ₫35–75 million a month and adds a certificate allowance of only ₫1,000,000 a month for N3 or ₫2,080,000 for N2 ([Reeracoen](https://www.reeracoen.com.vn/en/jobs/25754)). Japanese clients pay about **¥590,000 per person-month for a Vietnamese BrSE against ¥400,000 for a programmer**, roughly 48% more ([ISSOH](https://www.issoh.co.jp/column/details/16273/)). What separates the two roles is the ability to talk with the client, so speaking is the skill the market actually pays for.

Demand looks durable. Vietnam takes a little over 40% of Japanese companies' offshore development orders ([ISSOH](https://www.issoh.co.jp/column/details/16273/)). FPT's Japan revenue passed US$500 million in 2024 and grew another 25.4% in 2025 ([FPT](https://fpt.com/en/news/fpt-news/fpt-japan-surpasses-500-million-usd-in-revenue-targets-1-billion-usd-by-2027); [FPT 2025 results](https://fpt.com/en/news/fpts-profit-before-tax-of-vnd-13039-billion-representing-yoy-growth-of-178)). The strongest counterargument is that English will do. About half of the international developers in TokyoDev's 2025 survey rarely or never use Japanese at work ([TokyoDev 2025](https://2025.surveys.tokyodev.com/en-US/current-job/)), and Vietnamese IT postings requiring English rose 52.9% year on year in Q3 2025 ([ITviec](https://itviec.com/blog/thi-truong-tuyen-dung-it-viet-nam-quy-3-2025-thay-doi-yeu-cau-ky-nang/)). That English-friendly niche, however, favors senior engineers at foreign-owned firms. In the same survey, 83% of respondents who always use Japanese work at Japan-headquartered companies, and TokyoDev found almost no junior roles in Japan that need no Japanese ([TokyoDev](https://www.tokyodev.com/articles/first-job-programmer-japan)). Inside Japanese teams, language is the top worry: 62.8% of Japanese IT engineers who felt uneasy about foreign colleagues named the colleague's inability to speak Japanese ([Netshop Impress](https://netshop.impress.co.jp/node/14481)). For work in Japan itself, a rule in force since 15 April 2026 requires CEFR B2 proof, such as JLPT N2 or BJT 400+. It applies to some work-visa applicants at smaller employers whose jobs are mainly language-based work with people. Advisers say it generally does not cover engineers, but sources disagree on its scope ([Fragomen](https://www.fragomen.com/insights/japan-tightened-documentation-requirement-and-introduction-of-language-requirement-for-certain-work-visas.html); [Vialto Partners](https://vialtopartners.com/regional-alerts/japan-immigration-immigration-reform-2026-2029-key-changes-and-what-they-mean-for-employers-and-individuals)).

Why do learners who pass N3 still freeze? Skill-acquisition research treats speaking as a skill built by its own kind of practice. Comprehension practice mainly builds comprehension, and production practice builds production ([DeKeyser 2007](https://assets.cambridge.org/97805216/84040/excerpt/9780521684040_excerpt.pdf)). A second-language speaker also builds sentences slowly from explicitly learned rules until practice makes the process automatic ([Language Teaching research agenda](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/language-teaching/article/oral-fluency-in-a-second-language-a-research-agenda-for-the-next-ten-years/9C05358121F94757BE14049B2F4887FC/share/5db835c18522e3a8198bdc85d4e4de26c9352d34)). A meta-analysis of 30 studies found that production-based instruction **outperformed comprehension-based instruction on delayed tests of productive knowledge** ([Shintani, Li & Ellis 2013](https://espace.curtin.edu.au/handle/20.500.11937/54469)). Paul Nation's "four strands" principle gives roughly equal time to meaning-focused input, meaning-focused output, language-focused study and fluency practice ([Nation 2007](https://core.ac.uk/download/357330216.pdf)). JLPT preparation fills the input and language-focused strands and leaves the two speaking strands nearly empty, which describes someone who passes N3 and then freezes in conversation. Waiting does not help. The "silent period" has been documented mainly in children and is contested even there ([Wikipedia: Silent period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silent_period)). The adult case for delaying speech rests on old, thin evidence, such as a 1995 review reporting that learners whose speaking was delayed improved more in pronunciation, in studies whose design could not be checked ([ICPhS 1995](https://www.coli.uni-saarland.de/Phonetics/icphs/ICPhS1995/13_ICPhS_1995_Vol_1/p13.1_270.pdf)). Nor does speaking early lock in errors. Beginning learners of Japanese improved after implicit corrective feedback within a single study ([Iwashita 2003](https://cambridge.org/core/journals/studies-in-second-language-acquisition/article/negative-feedback-and-positive-evidence-in-taskbased-interaction/7A1B2BADFD35078D677D98C6D81569B6)), and the effects of feedback last to delayed tests ([Lyster & Saito 2010](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/4999EE1C8379B2BF026B148EAF373CA1)). After years of input-heavy study, this learner's risk is not errors that have set. It is knowledge that has never been used to speak.

## Japanese IT hiring runs on about ten rehearsable routines

The speaking that gets an engineer hired is much narrower than "conversation". A Japanese mid-career interview typically moves through five question types: 自己紹介 (self-introduction), 転職理由 (reason for changing jobs), 志望動機 (motivation), 自己PR (self-promotion) and 逆質問 (the candidate's own questions). doda recommends a self-introduction of **about one minute, roughly 300 characters**, covering name, career, a strength and motivation ([doda](https://doda.jp/guide/mensetsu/interview/); [doda engineer guide](https://doda.jp/engineer/guide/it/004.html)). HR interviewers check business basics and fit, while engineers from the team check skill match and willingness to learn ([Mynavi IT Agent](https://mynavi-agent.jp/it/knowhow/system_interview/)). For interviews at Japanese companies in Vietnam, CareerLink describes rounds of experience questions, a conversation with a Japanese manager and a trial translation. It advises using です/ます throughout and giving one concrete example instead of a theoretical answer ([CareerLink](https://www.careerlink.vn/cam-nang-viec-lam/?p=31818)). A Vietnamese BrSE mentorship recap calls 自己PR "the most important part" ([Viblo](https://viblo.asia/p/bridge-to-japan-brse-mentorship-recap-brse-fresher-va-cach-tao-an-tuong-voi-nha-tuyen-dung-EvbLbOQbVnk)). Career guides add two tactics that suit rehearsal. Put a short episode inside each answer, so the interviewer's follow-up questions land on prepared ground ([Axxis](https://axxis.co.jp/magazine/56947)). Keep every answer consistent with the CV ([VietnamWorks](https://www.vietnamworks.com/hrinsider/cac-cau-hoi-phong-van-cua-cong-ty-nhat-thuong-gap.html)). Even the ritual has set phrases. On entering: 「○○と申します。本日はお時間を取っていただき、ありがとうございます。よろしくお願いします」. At the end: 「本日はお時間をいただき、誠にありがとうございました」 ([Townwork](https://townwork.net/magazine/?p=85848); [kotora](https://www.kotora.jp/c/98706-2)).

Daily work after hiring is just as patterned. Vietnamese BrSEs say client communication mostly comes down to 報連相 (hōrensō: report, inform, consult), with keigo adjusted to each client ([Viblo](https://viblo.asia/p/giao-tiep-voi-nguoi-nhat-va-kinh-nghiem-khi-giao-tiep-voi-khach-hang-nhat-trong-du-an-dWrvwdOVRw38)). Japanese business guides set conventions for each routine, summarized below. Together, these routines are a practice syllabus.

| Routine | Where it is tested | What a good version contains | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| 自己紹介 (self-introduction) | Opening of every interview | About one minute (~300 characters): name, career, a strength or achievement, motivation | [doda](https://doda.jp/guide/mensetsu/interview/) |
| 自己PR and a past-project explanation | Core of the interview; engineers dig into it | One concrete episode that invites follow-up; specific and positive, without piling on jargon | [Axxis](https://axxis.co.jp/magazine/56947); [kotora](https://www.kotora.jp/c/78978-2/) |
| 転職理由, 志望動機, 逆質問 | Middle and close of the interview | Consistent with the CV; tied to the company's work and values | [VietnamWorks](https://www.vietnamworks.com/hrinsider/cac-cau-hoi-phong-van-cua-cong-ty-nhat-thuong-gap.html); [OneCareer](https://onecareer.jp/articles/3601) |
| 朝会 and 進捗報告 (stand-up, progress or delay report) | Daily work | Reported early, conclusion first, facts kept apart from guesses (marked 〜と考えられます), with a proposed fix and the decision needed | [atsoho](https://atsoho.com/blog/progress-delays-report); [Asana](https://asana.com/ja/resources/what-is-business-progress-report) |
| 仕様確認 and 認識合わせ (clarifying a spec, aligning understanding) | Client Q&A and the QA表 (Q&A sheet) | Narrow questions that offer options; 復唱; say exactly what to check, the scope and the deadline | [Classmethod](https://dev.classmethod.jp/articles/confirmation-talk-and-repeat/); [shopowner-support](https://www.shopowner-support.net/?p=265082); [atsoho](https://atsoho.com/blog/check-requests-email-external) |
| 障害の第一報 (incident first report) | Outages | Sent before the cause is known: start time, what is affected, current action, time of the next report; one guide targets 30 minutes for high-impact outages | [ISSOH](https://www.issoh.co.jp/column/details/19774/); [Zenn](https://zenn.dev/midori/articles/6ed7a1fca87d86) |

In these routines, "good enough" means passing on the meaning accurately more than sounding polished. Vietnamese BrSEs say a BrSE's language ability means grasping what the client actually needs rather than translating word for word ([Viblo](https://viblo.asia/p/ky-nang-ve-ngon-ngu-can-co-cua-brse-xu-ly-thong-tin-va-truyen-dat-thong-tin-XL6lANxJ5ek)). An experienced comtor warns that a request phrased like a question can be an urgent instruction, which literal translation misses ([Viblo](https://viblo.asia/p/mot-vai-kinh-nghiem-lam-nghe-comtor-maGK7q4Olj2)). A 2022 report on a Japanese course for intermediate to upper-intermediate Vietnamese BrSEs and IT communicators found them struggling with **both using Japanese and passing information on** ([Le Bich Diep 2022](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jlem/28/2/28_40/_article/-char/ja)). An offshore vendor's column traces many offshore defects to specs misunderstood in translation ([Shift Asia](https://shiftasia.com/ja/column/%e3%82%aa%e3%83%95%e3%82%b7%e3%83%a7%e3%82%a2%e9%96%8b%e7%99%ba%e3%81%ae%e5%93%81%e8%b3%aa%e8%aa%b2%e9%a1%8c%e3%81%a8%e3%82%b3%e3%83%9f%e3%83%a5%e3%83%8b%e3%82%b1%e3%83%bc%e3%82%b7%e3%83%a7%e3%83%b3/)). A former Mercari engineer adds that a short summary of the issue plus a concrete plan beats a detailed status list ([TokyoDev](https://www.tokyodev.com/articles/working-and-communicating-with-japanese-engineers)). For someone without daily conversation this is good news. Workplace Japanese is a closed set of routines built from fixed phrases, and that is exactly the kind of material repetition can make automatic. Rehearsing these scenes should earn more hiring value per hour than open-ended chat, although no study has compared the two directly.

## Feedback, planning and repetition outperform free talk and shadowing

Speaking research supports a short list of techniques, and they are not the ones self-taught learners usually try first. The table grades each one by the weight of its evidence. "Strong" means several meta-analyses agree. "Limited" means a few small or uncontrolled studies.

| Technique | Best evidence | Effect | Mainly improves | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Oral corrective feedback | [Lyster & Saito 2010](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/4999EE1C8379B2BF026B148EAF373CA1); [Li 2010](https://researchspace.auckland.ac.nz/handle/2292/24985?show=full) | Medium, lasting to delayed tests; prompts beat recasts in classrooms | Grammatical accuracy | Strong |
| Interaction with a partner | [Mackey & Goo 2007](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/59863/) (28 studies) | d = 0.71 immediate, 1.09 delayed (figures quoted by other papers) | Grammar, vocabulary | Strong–moderate |
| Explicit pronunciation teaching | [Lee, Jang & Plonsky 2015](https://experts.nau.edu/en/publications/the-effectiveness-of-second-language-pronunciation-instruction-a-/) (86 studies); [Saito & Plonsky 2019](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/10156232/1/OASIS_Summary_Saito_and_Plonsky_2019.pdf) | d = 0.80; weaker on overall accent | Specific sounds, intelligibility | Strong |
| Planning before speaking; speaking without time pressure | [Johnson & Abdi Tabari](https://english.ecu.edu/?p=13678) | Medium for complexity and fluency; large for accuracy and complexity | Depends on condition | Moderate–strong |
| Task repetition and 4/3/2 | [de Jong & Perfetti 2011](https://www.lrdc.pitt.edu/perfettilab/pubpdfs/de%20Jong%20Language%20Learning.pdf); [Thai & Boers 2016](https://zendy.io/title/10.1002/tesq.232) (Vietnamese learners) | Reliable fluency gains; accuracy gains only at constant time | Fluency | Moderate |
| AI or dialogue-system partner | [Hou & Min 2025](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/31847710516602398819C5E594038E7B); [Bibauw et al. 2022](https://www.lltjournal.org/item-detail/1035) | g = 0.61; d ≈ 0.6 (mostly systems that predate today's chatbots) | Speaking overall | Moderate |
| Spaced recall practice | [Kim & Webb 2022](https://eld.bl.uk/catalog/100152978253_0x000001) (48 experiments) | Medium-to-large spacing effect | Word recall | Strong for vocabulary |
| Formulaic sequences (chunks) | [Boers et al. 2006](https://repository.uantwerpen.be/link/irua/59173); [Tavakoli & Uchihara 2020](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/86157) | Small trials and correlational studies | Fluency, perceived proficiency | Limited–moderate |
| Shadowing | [Systematic review of 44 studies](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:3104cae1-3a6b-400a-b173-de44384238d2); small studies of Japanese learners | No pooled effect | Listening, rhythm, intonation | Limited |
| Self-transcription | [Salas 2015](https://revistas.udistrital.edu.co/index.php/calj/article/download/4989/10682/43671) | Learners noticed about 25% of their errors | Noticing errors | Limited |

Corrective feedback is the anchor. A classroom meta-analysis of 15 studies (N = 827) found feedback effective and lasting. **Prompts, which push learners to fix their own errors, beat recasts**, the quiet corrected rewording that teachers usually give ([Lyster & Saito 2010](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/4999EE1C8379B2BF026B148EAF373CA1)). In the classic classroom study, recasts made up 55% of all feedback, yet they were the least likely to lead learners to fix the error ([Lyster & Ranta 1997](https://escholarship.mcgill.ca/downloads/0p096b851)). There are caveats. Recasts beat prompts for grammatically complex features ([Schenck 2020](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1186/s40862-020-00097-9.pdf)). Explicit feedback works faster, while implicit feedback lasts longer ([Li 2010](https://researchspace.auckland.ac.nz/handle/2292/24985?show=full)). Feedback given at the end of an activity shows no clear difference from immediate feedback ([Xu & Zeng 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1026174/full)). For learners of Japanese, Iwashita found that hearing correct models helped only learners who were already above average, while implicit corrective feedback helped at every level ([Iwashita 2003](https://cambridge.org/core/journals/studies-in-second-language-acquisition/article/negative-feedback-and-positive-evidence-in-taskbased-interaction/7A1B2BADFD35078D677D98C6D81569B6)). That argues for conversation with correction over listening alone. In practice, ask a tutor to prompt on forms you already studied for the JLPT, recast new or complex ones, and leave time at the end for a list of errors.

Repetition and planning decide which side of speech improves. Learners who gave the same talk in four, then three, then two minutes (the "4/3/2" technique) made larger fluency gains than learners who spoke on different topics ([de Jong & Perfetti 2011](https://www.lrdc.pitt.edu/perfettilab/pubpdfs/de%20Jong%20Language%20Learning.pdf)). The most relevant study used 20 Vietnamese learners of English. Under shrinking time, fluency improved most, but accuracy and complexity did not change. Three deliveries at constant time gave smaller fluency gains plus modest gains in complexity and accuracy. Learners also reused much of their wording word for word ([Thai & Boers 2016](https://zendy.io/title/10.1002/tesq.232); [UCL citing paper](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/10128571/1/LTR2021.pdf)). Planning works the same way. Planning before a task has medium effects on complexity and fluency, and speaking with no time limit has large effects on complexity and accuracy ([Johnson & Abdi Tabari](https://english.ecu.edu/?p=13678)). The rule is to switch conditions: time pressure for fluency, no time limit for accuracy. Always speaking as fast as possible trains only fluency.

Fixed phrases link rehearsal to real conversation. Learners taught to notice standard word combinations were rated more proficient by judges who did not know which group they were in ([Boers et al. 2006](https://repository.uantwerpen.be/link/irua/59173)). Speakers who use more frequent multiword sequences articulate faster and pause less often inside clauses ([Tavakoli & Uchihara 2020](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/86157)). Work Japanese is full of such chunks, from 「ご確認いただけますでしょうか」 to 「認識に相違がないかご確認ください」 ([Luft](https://www.luft.co.jp/media/confirmation-rephrasing/); [atsoho](https://atsoho.com/blog/check-requests-email-external)), so a bank of these phrases, drilled aloud, is cheap and well aimed. The direction of the drill matters. Recall drills beat recognition drills for words you need to produce ([IRAL study](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/iral-2015-0022/html?lang=en)). Spaced practice has a medium-to-large effect on second-language learning, and shorter gaps do worse on delayed tests ([Kim & Webb 2022](https://eld.bl.uk/catalog/100152978253_0x000001)). Flashcards therefore build speaking best when you see a Vietnamese or English cue and say the Japanese aloud before flipping.

Several popular methods belong in a supporting role. Shadowing has no meta-analysis, and a systematic review of 44 studies found methodological weaknesses ([Oxford ORA](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:3104cae1-3a6b-400a-b173-de44384238d2)). Its clearest benefit is listening, for lower-proficiency learners ([TESL Ontario](https://contact.teslontario.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/Hamada-Shadowing.pdf)). A dissertation on learners of Japanese found no significant gains in speed or in self-correction fluency, only fewer silent pauses inside sentences ([Sakaue, Purdue](https://docs.lib.purdue.edu/dissertations/AAI30506257)). For this learner its real value is prosody, covered in the next section. Transcribing your own recordings helps you notice errors, but in one study learners caught only about 25% of them ([Salas 2015](https://revistas.udistrital.edu.co/index.php/calj/article/download/4989/10682/43671)), so a second pair of ears is needed. Training in communication strategies raised oral test scores in one study of Japanese learners of English ([Nakatani 2005, as cited](https://www.nufs.ac.jp/media/Junko2018.pdf)) but had little overall effect in another ([Dörnyei 1995, as cited](https://tsukuba.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/40997/files/JACTAT%20Bruno.pdf)). AI conversation partners have moderate support, **g = 0.61** for speaking across 16 studies ([Hou & Min 2025](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/31847710516602398819C5E594038E7B)). A 2026 synthesis of 67 studies, presented at a conference but not yet published, found larger effects on delayed tests (g = 0.92) than on immediate ones ([Wang et al. 2026](https://research.dial.uclouvain.be/entities/publication/29791a54-111b-40eb-bacb-346494980619)). Most of the systems studied, however, were rule-based, not today's generative voice assistants.

Taken together, the evidence describes a loop rather than a menu. Plan a task for a minute or two from keyword notes, deliver it three times under shrinking time while recording, and get corrections from a tutor or an error list from an AI. Perform it again the next day with no time limit, aiming for accuracy, then come back to it after a week and again after two or three weeks. No study has tested this exact sequence in Japanese, but each step rests on the findings above, and most of it can be done alone. Talking to yourself in the target language improved speaking fluency in a 2023 study, most of all under increasing time limits ([NTNU](https://scholar.lib.ntnu.edu.tw/zh/publications/%E5%A6%82%E4%BD%95-%E7%B7%B4%E7%BF%92-%E8%88%87%E8%87%AA%E5%B7%B1%E5%B0%8D%E8%A9%B1-%E5%88%A9%E7%94%A8-soliloquizing-%E6%8F%90%E5%8D%87%E5%A4%96%E8%AA%9E%E5%AD%B8%E7%BF%92%E8%80%85%E5%8F%A3%E8%AA%9E%E6%B5%81%E5%88%A9%E5%BA%A6%E4%B9%8B%E6%9C%80%E4%BD%B3%E6%93%8D%E4%BD%9C%E7%92%B0%E5%A2%83/)). Reading aloud combined with extensive reading improved fluency, where extensive reading alone did not ([KAKEN report](https://kaken.nii.ac.jp/ja/file/KAKENHI-PROJECT-23H05056/23H05056seika.pdf)).

## Hán Việt speeds vocabulary, but long vowels, flat pitch and keigo need work

Japanese research on Vietnamese learners keeps finding the same pronunciation problems. The special morae (long vowels, double consonants, ん) are hard, and **long vowels are the hardest** ([Tanaka, JAFLE Bulletin](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jaflebulletin/26/0/26_20/_pdf/-char/en)). In listening tests, learners heard a short vowel said on a low pitch as a long one ([VNU-ULIS](https://jfs.ulis.vnu.edu.vn/index.php/fs/article/view/11)). They found high-low accent words harder to tell apart ([VNU-ULIS](https://jfs.ulis.vnu.edu.vn/index.php/fs/article/view/1069)), and when they said the wrong accent they also tended to lengthen vowels wrongly ([CiNii](https://cir.nii.ac.jp/crid/1390572174440534912)). In speech, long vowels get cut (じょうしゃ becomes じょしゃ) and vowels drop where two meet (せいりけん becomes せりけん), because learners fit Japanese into Vietnamese syllable rhythm ([PSJ 2018](https://conference.wdc-jp.com/psj/2018/contents/common/doc/P09.pdf)). Their vowel length and loudness vary far less than in native speech ([Matsuda et al. 2019](https://www.hituzi.co.jp/epublish/academic_journal/nhng_onsei/nihongoonsei007_nhg_06matsuda_ab.pdf)). Pitch habits carried over from Vietnamese tones give a high, flat delivery with sudden jumps at the start of phrases ([Matsuda 2016](http://www.acras.jp/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/55a243da409e42383ec120498cee00b7.pdf)). Vietnamese learners' speech was rated less natural than native speech, and it often showed throat tension that sounds strained ([academia.edu study](https://www.academia.edu/104755876/%E3%83%A9%E3%83%BC%E3%83%8B%E3%83%B3%E3%82%B0%E3%83%92%E3%82%B9%E3%83%88%E3%83%AA%E3%83%BC%E3%81%8B%E3%82%89%E8%A6%8B%E3%81%9F%E3%83%99%E3%83%88%E3%83%8A%E3%83%A0%E4%BA%BA%E6%97%A5%E6%9C%AC%E8%AA%9E%E5%AD%A6%E7%BF%92%E8%80%85%E3%81%AE%E7%99%BA%E9%9F%B3%E7%BF%92%E5%BE%97%E3%82%B9%E3%83%88%E3%83%A9%E3%83%86%E3%82%B8%E3%83%BC%E3%81%AE%E7%A0%94%E7%A9%B6)). Consonant problems are fewer and often depend on the dialect. ザ/ジャ/ヤ mix-ups are usually explained by Vietnamese treating z- and y-type sounds as one, said [z] in the North and [j] in the South ([PSJ 2018 B3](https://conference.wdc-jp.com/psj/2018/contents/common/doc/B3.pdf)). Learners also confuse ラ, ナ and ダ ([TUFS](https://www.coelang.tufs.ac.jp/mt/ja/pmod/practical/01-02-01.php)) and struggle to devoice the す in です/ます ([JLEM 22](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jlem/22/1/22_KJ00009846945/_article/-char/ja/)). In Matsuda's doctoral survey, learners did not think Japanese pronunciation was especially difficult ([Matsuda thesis](https://gensha.hit-u.ac.jp/archives/theses-archive/theses/89a.pdf)), so judging your own pronunciation will miss these problems.

These problems are not cosmetic. In a listening experiment, prosody shaped native speakers' ratings of learner speech more than individual sounds did, and pitch mattered most (Sato 1995, as cited in [音声研究 2019](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/onseikenkyu/23/0/23_6/_pdf)). Words said with the wrong accent were recognized less accurately, and accent errors hurt word recognition more than sound errors did ([Ariga, LSJ](https://www.ls-japan.org/modules/documents/LSJpapers/meeting/168/handouts/b/B-3_168.pdf)). When native raters marked the spots in learners' speech that sounded odd, they picked word-accent errors, flat tone and sudden pitch drops, the same habits Vietnamese learners carry ([Nunomura 2024](https://www.tufs.ac.jp/ts/personal/ykawa/kaken2023/20240124_nunomura.pdf)). Pronunciation teaching works in general, with **d = 0.80** across 86 studies ([Lee, Jang & Plonsky 2015](https://experts.nau.edu/en/publications/the-effectiveness-of-second-language-pronunciation-instruction-a-/)), though it works more clearly on specific sounds than on overall accent ([Saito & Plonsky 2019](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/10156232/1/OASIS_Summary_Saito_and_Plonsky_2019.pdf)). Listening training on sound contrasts also improves speaking, with d = 0.54 for production ([Sakai & Moorman 2018](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/57401D28450902EE96659AD10AA11488)). Accent work pays off twice. Among Chinese learners of Japanese, the ability to notice accent errors predicted listening scores ([Tamaoka](https://tamaoka.org/scholarly/sadokuari/2016/143.pdf)).

For Vietnamese learners, teachers advise working on rhythm before consonants ([kanjifumi](https://www.kanjifumi.jp/seicho/20655/)). The approaches tried with them include saying morae slowly and at equal length ([Matsuda, ALCE](https://alce.jp/meeting/07/pre1pp.pdf)), and accent-focused training to fix vowel length ([Tanaka](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jaflebulletin/26/0/26_20/_pdf/-char/en)). One exploratory study found that accent lines showing the height of each mora made correct pronunciation more likely ([PSJ 2019](https://conference.wdc-jp.com/psj/2019/contents/common/doc/B1.pdf)). Training with sound waveforms on screen improved how learners of Japanese produced double consonants ([Motohashi-Saigo & Hardison 2009](https://scholarspace.manoa.hawaii.edu/bitstream/10125/44179/1/13_02_motohashisaigohardison.pdf)). The free workhorse is the University of Tokyo's **OJAD with its Suzuki-kun prosody tutor**. Type any Japanese text and it marks the accent, draws the predicted pitch curve and reads the text aloud with synthetic speech. Its guesses are sometimes wrong, and you can correct them by hand ([OJAD](https://www.gavo.t.u-tokyo.ac.jp/ojad/pages/usage); [Japan Foundation](https://www.jpf.go.jp/j/project/japanese/teach/tsushin/hint/201810.html)). That gives a direct pipeline for interview material: write the self-introduction, run it through Suzuki-kun, shadow the model, then record yourself and compare. For looking up accents, learner communities favor the NHK accent dictionary in the paid Monokakido app ([xythh](https://xythh.github.io/resources)). Dogen's paid Japanese Phonetics course, taught in English, covers pitch accent in about 80 lessons ([Tofugu](https://www.tofugu.com/reviews/dogen-japanese-phonetics/)).

This is where shadowing earns its place, as a prosody tool. In one study of learners of Japanese, errors on the special morae fell by more than 60% ([Ryukoku](https://opac.ryukoku.ac.jp/iwjs0005opc/bdyview.do?bodyid=BD00003557&elmid=Body&fname=KJ00004859782.pdf)). In another, long vowels, devoicing and intonation improved over time, though its author warns not to overstate the gains ([Tanabe 2014](https://www.glocal.gifu-u.ac.jp/docs/2014_tanabe.pdf)). A small Vietnamese study found shadowing helped with prosodic problems including speaking rate ([academia.edu study](https://www.academia.edu/104755876/%E3%83%A9%E3%83%BC%E3%83%8B%E3%83%B3%E3%82%B0%E3%83%92%E3%82%B9%E3%83%88%E3%83%AA%E3%83%BC%E3%81%8B%E3%82%89%E8%A6%8B%E3%81%9F%E3%83%99%E3%83%88%E3%83%8A%E3%83%A0%E4%BA%BA%E6%97%A5%E6%9C%AC%E8%AA%9E%E5%AD%A6%E7%BF%92%E8%80%85%E3%81%AE%E7%99%BA%E9%9F%B3%E7%BF%92%E5%BE%97%E3%82%B9%E3%83%88%E3%83%A9%E3%83%86%E3%82%B8%E3%83%BC%E3%81%AE%E7%A0%94%E7%A9%B6)). Kuroshio Shuppan's 『シャドーイング 日本語を話そう！就職・アルバイト・進学面接編』 drills job-interview answers, especially 志望動機, and its 2023 audio-download edition comes with **Vietnamese translations** ([版元ドットコム](https://www.hanmoto.com/bd/isbn/9784874249468)). 『シャドーイング もっと話せる日本語 初〜中級編』 also has Vietnamese and suits the N4–N3 stage ([楽天ブックス](https://books.rakuten.co.jp/rb/17553227/)). Use audio you almost fully understand. Follow the standard steps: listen, mumble along, check the meaning in the text, read along with the audio, shadow while copying the rhythm and pitch, and finally shadow while following the meaning ([eigovis](https://eigovis.net/books/shadowing-entry.htm)).

Vocabulary is where Vietnamese speakers hold an edge. About **50% of the two-kanji words and 25% of all words in JLPT vocabulary lists** match or resemble Hán Việt words. The matches cluster in late-intermediate to advanced written vocabulary, and the benefit is likely largest for the technical terms that IT work uses ([Matsuda 2008](https://researchmap.jp/read0103233/published_papers/1795870)). Early beginners barely use this link, while intermediate learners use it to guess the meaning of kanji words ([Hoang & Tamaoka 2021](https://koidekinen.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/2021_hoangtamaoka.pdf)). The sound correspondences help pronunciation too. Middle Chinese -ng survives as -ng or -nh in Hán Việt and became a long vowel in Japanese on'yomi ([Wikipedia: Sino-Xenic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Xenic_pronunciations)). So 環境 hoàn cảnh is かんきょう, while a final -c maps to -ku, as in 確認 xác nhận → かくにん ([tiengnhatmoingay](https://tiengnhatmoingay.com/chuyen-tu-am-han-viet-sang-am-on/)). That gives the learner a rule for exactly the long vowels they tend to cut. Two cautions apply. Hán Việt tones say nothing about Japanese pitch, so each word's accent still has to be looked up. And false friends wait in self-introductions. Vietnamese học sinh (学生) means a school pupil rather than a university student, and giám đốc (監督) means a company director, which Japanese 監督 does not ([Wikipedia: 漢越語](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E6%BC%A2%E8%B6%8A%E8%AA%9E); [langhacks](https://langhacks.com/vietnamese-han-viet/)). Check IT vocabulary in a dictionary the same way before it goes into a script.

The deepest gap is in politeness and conversation habits. Vietnamese shows politeness mainly through forms of address and a sentence-final ạ ([vietomato](https://vietomato.com/cach-noi-kinh-ngu)). That maps onto です/ます, but not onto the humble forms (申す, 参る, いたす, おる) that fill self-introductions and reports. The government's 敬語の指針 (keigo guidelines) sorts keigo into five types ([文化庁](https://www.bunka.go.jp/seisaku/bunkashingikai/kokugo/hokoku/pdf/keigo_tosin.pdf)). The overused 「させていただく」 fits only when you act with the listener's permission and benefit from it ([Indeed Japan](https://jp.indeed.com/career-advice/career-development/have-the-privilege-of-doing)). Vietnamese picks its words for "I" and "you" by age and relationship, mostly from kinship terms ([TUFS](https://www.coelang.tufs.ac.jp/mt/vi/gmod/courses/c01/lesson08/step02/explanation/015.html)). Carried into Japanese, the habit of naming the listener produces あなた, which can sound distant or rude to a superior ([word-dictionary.jp](https://word-dictionary.jp/posts/6065/)). Too few あいづち (listener responses such as はい and そうですね) is another trap. Indonesian learners who gave few of them were taken as not listening ([Osaka Univ.](https://ir.library.osaka-u.ac.jp/repo/ouka/all/54503/sjlc25_133.pdf)), and an anecdotal account says basic Vietnamese backchanneling is a はい-type word ([note](https://note.com/vietomato/n/ne6daf7640906)). Intermediate and advanced learners rate requests the hardest of five common speech acts ([JLEM 26](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jlem/26/1/26_48/_pdf)). Keigo can be measured rather than guessed: ALC's JSST speaking test scores register and politeness (待遇表現) as a separate criterion ([ALC JSST](https://www.alc-education.co.jp/business/jsst/)).

## Tutors supply feedback, AI supplies volume, a first job supplies immersion

Paid one-to-one lessons are the most dependable source of correction from Vietnam. In November 2026 listings, community tutors on italki and Preply charge roughly **US$8–20 an hour** and professional teachers about US$30–40. italki's own guide estimates US$35–80 for business-Japanese specialists ([italki blog](https://italki.com/en/blog/japanese-tutor-cost); [italki listings](https://www.italki.com/en/teachers/japanese?page=33); [Preply](https://preply.com/en/online/japanese-tutors?page=5)). Some tutors advertise mock interviews, 自己PR coaching and a background in IT consulting ([italki profile](https://www.italki.com/ko/teacher/20789638/japanese)). Two lessons a week with a community tutor cost about US$90–170 a month. A sensible mix is a cheap tutor for volume, plus a specialist once or twice a month for mock interviews. Video lessons lose little. Online live conversation supports language development as well as face-to-face conversation does ([Ziegler 2016](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/studies-in-second-language-acquisition/article/abs/synchronous-computermediated-communication-and-interaction/F05E29E42457059532258158B93167D7)), and because Japan is two hours ahead, Japanese tutors' 19:00–23:00 evening slots fall at 17:00–21:00 in Vietnam. Run each lesson like a training session. Send the target task and keigo in advance. Ask for corrections typed in the chat. Repeat the task two or three times with a shrinking time limit. Open the next lesson by doing it again.

AI voice partners supply cheap, unlimited practice with no one to be judged by. ChatGPT Go launched in Vietnam in October 2025 and costs about 132,000 VND a month including VAT ([Tuoi Tre](https://news.tuoitre.vn/chatgpt-go-launches-in-vietnam-as-openai-expands-affordable-access-in-asia-103251012103426691.htm); [Zemith](https://www.zemith.com/en/learn/chatgpt-price-vietnam)). Gemini Live has spoken Japanese since October 2024 and is free in the mobile app, with usage limits ([Google Japan](https://blog.google/intl/ja-jp/company-news/technology/gemini-live/); [Money Forward](https://biz.moneyforward.com/ai/basic/869/)). Claude's voice mode added Japanese in July 2026 ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/07/23/anthropic-upgrades-claude-voice-mode-with-more-powerful-models/)). Structured apps such as Speak, at about US$99 a year, add scripted role-plays ([Preply blog](https://preply.com/en/blog/best-japanese-speaking-app/)).

Their weak points are the same ones Vietnamese learners most need help with. A reviewer deliberately mispronounced a Japanese phrase and Gemini said it was correct ([Xataka](https://www.xatakandroid.com/aplicaciones-android/arigato-gozaimasu-gemini-estoy-aprendiendo-japones-ia-genial-para-practicar-corregir-mi-pronunciacion)). Speech recognizers, Whisper among them, tend to silently correct learners' wrong verb and noun forms, at least in English learner speech ([LET Journal](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/letcj/35/0/35_15/_article/-char/en)). At least one role-play app says outright that it does not score pitch accent ([Jenova](https://www.jenova.ai/en/resources/ai-learn-japanese-through-roleplay-guide)). Even purpose-built speech-recognition practice helps individual sounds more than prosody ([Ngo, Chen & Lai 2024](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/recall/article/effectiveness-of-automatic-speech-recognition-in-eslefl-pronunciation-a-metaanalysis/A915444CF252B61D14961D2FE733822D)). A Japanese instructor judged ChatGPT's voice mode "definitely not ready for lower level learners", noting that it cannot show text while talking ([Substack](https://jaetakeuchi.substack.com/p/ai-voice-mode-for-language-learners)). So use AI for rehearsal at volume: daily role-plays of interview questions, stand-ups and delay reports, with the register set at the start and a request for an error list at the end. Leave judgments about pronunciation and keigo to people and to OJAD.

Language exchange is free and builds fluency and confidence, but not accuracy. In a semester-long video exchange, 30 US learners of Japanese became easier to understand, mostly because they spoke faster ([Akiyama & Saito 2016](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/10066023/1/MLJ2016.pdf)). A Japanese–Australian student exchange reduced speaking anxiety ([Tabuchi & Kobayashi 2024](https://cir.nii.ac.jp/crid/1390019900049473280?lang=en)). On HelloTalk and Tandem, though, corrections are rare and users often report harassment ([Trustpilot](https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/www.hellotalk.com?page=2); [FluentU](https://www.fluentu.com/blog/tandem-review/)). A Vietnamese speaker has an unusual advantage here. Japanese residents learning Vietnamese are a less crowded pool of partners: one school in Vietnam that teaches Vietnamese to Japanese people reports more than 1,500 graduates since 2007, about 70% of its students business people working locally ([studychain](https://studychain.jp/interview/zengaikokugokyouikucenter/)).

In-person options cluster in Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City. The Japan Foundation library in Hanoi costs 60,000 VND a year ([JF Hanoi](https://hn.jpf.go.jp/membership)). The 11th Japan Vietnam Festival in HCMC had more than 300 booths in March 2026 ([Tuoi Tre](https://news.tuoitre.vn/japan-vietnam-festival-back-in-ho-chi-minh-city-on-largest-ever-scale-103260227121953793.htm)), and HCMC has Japanese-specific meetup groups ([Meetup](https://secure.meetup.com/find/vn--thanh-pho-ho-chi-minh/meet-new-friends)). The Japanese chambers of commerce in Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City have about 1,900 member companies between them ([JETRO](https://www.jetro.go.jp/world/asia/vn/basic_01.html)), and that corporate presence points to the strongest substitute for immersion: a job. An N3-level developer or tester role on a Japan-facing team means using Japanese every day, with real consequences and real feedback.

Order the channels by how much is at stake. How competent learners *feel*, not how anxious they are, is the strongest predictor of willingness to speak ([Shirvan et al. 2019](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s10936-019-09656-9); [Jin & Lee 2022](https://sage.figshare.com/collections/Willingness_to_Communicate_and_its_High-Evidence_Factors_A_Meta-Analytic_Structural_Equation_Modeling_Approach/5950697)). Anxiety still matters: it correlates with lower achievement at r = −.36 ([Teimouri, Goetze & Plonsky 2019](https://experts.nau.edu/en/publications/second-language-anxiety-and-achievement/)). In a study of anxious learners, chatbot practice mainly reduced the fear of being judged ([NCCU thesis](https://nccur.lib.nccu.edu.tw/handle/140.119/158726)). A 2026 review finds that most chatbot studies report lower anxiety, but the effect varies from person to person ([SHS Web of Conferences](https://www.shs-conferences.org/articles/shsconf/pdf/2026/09/shsconf_icepcc2026_02014.pdf)). A sensible ladder runs from solo recordings to AI role-plays, then a tutor, then exchange partners and mock interviews, then real interviews. Rehearse each set piece until it is fluent before trying it on a person. Learners' own reports point the same way. One learner who logged 4,400 hours with only about 150 hours of speaking said their confidence grew once they accepted simpler sentences ([r/LearnJapanese](https://reddit.rtrace.io/r/LearnJapanese/comments/1du8dje/4400_hours_over_4_years_results_as_a_normal/lbybpn8/?context=3)). A poster on a Vietnamese tech forum says that AI simulates conversation well, but the key is to open your mouth and practice every day ([Tinhte](https://tinhte.vn/thread/v.4168263/)).

### Eight hours a week, half of them spoken

No study has found a minimum effective dose of speaking practice, so the budget below applies principles rather than tested numbers. For a working engineer studying about eight hours a week, Nation's four strands suggest about two hours each, with language-focused study kept to about a quarter ([Nation 2007](https://core.ac.uk/download/357330216.pdf); [Wikipedia: Paul Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Nation)). Spacing research favors many short sessions that return to the same material after growing gaps ([Kim & Webb 2022](https://eld.bl.uk/catalog/100152978253_0x000001)). For adults, packing the same hours into fewer weeks did as well as spreading them out, or better ([Serrano & Muñoz 2007](https://diposit.ub.edu/items/457c4ebe-8087-4511-9f7a-044a352b2fd2); [Serrano 2010](https://diposit.ub.edu/dspace/handle/2445/164085)). Both findings favor practicing every day over cramming at weekends.

| Strand | Weekly time | What fills it for this learner |
|---|---|---|
| Meaning-focused input | About 2 hours | JLPT N3 listening and reading; an episode of Rebuild.fm or fukabori.fm; a DroidKaigi or PyCon JP talk |
| Language-focused study (keep to about a quarter) | About 2 hours | JLPT grammar and vocabulary; OJAD pitch work on your own scripts; humble keigo forms; reviewing the error log |
| Meaning-focused output | About 2 hours | Two tutor lessons (one mock interview, one spec-clarification or delay-report role-play), plus AI role-plays on other days |
| Fluency development | About 2 hours, as 15–20 minutes a day | 4/3/2 delivery of one set piece; shadowing audio you already understand; recalling the phrase bank aloud |

The daily fluency minutes can rotate. On day one, give one set piece 4/3/2 while recording. On day two, give the same piece at a constant pace for accuracy and shadow for ten minutes. On day three, recall the phrase bank aloud. The dose can be small: one shadowing study saw a noticeable change from two 10–15-minute sessions a week for a month ([TESL Ontario](https://contact.teslontario.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/Hamada-Shadowing.pdf)). Several books give the practice structure. The Japan Foundation's free いろどり course offers A1–A2 everyday speaking tasks with Vietnamese support ([Japan Foundation](https://www.jpf.go.jp/j/project/japanese/teach/tsushin/news/202104.html)). 3A Network's 『会話に挑戦！』 has learners repeat each role-play with the situation changed, which is task repetition in textbook form ([books.or.jp](https://www.books.or.jp/book-details/9784883193615)). ALC's 『しごとの日本語 IT業務編』 follows conversations through the system-development process, although it dates from 2008, so treat its conversations as frames and update the technical terms ([アルク](https://book.alc.co.jp/book/b10029424.html)). The newer 『IT業務・日本語会話トレーニング』 (2022) teaches IT Japanese through skits ([Japan Foundation booklist](https://www.jpf.go.jp/j/urawa/j_library/booklist/dl/BL16-2023.pdf)). Natural engineer-to-engineer Japanese comes free from podcasts such as Rebuild.fm and fukabori.fm ([Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/jp/podcast/id603013428); [Rephonic](https://rephonic.com/podcasts/fukaborifm)) and conference talks such as DroidKaigi's on YouTube ([Zenn](https://zenn.dev/fukurow/articles/0a591eb22b3106)). It is more casual than client-facing keigo, so it complements the business material rather than replacing it.

### Track speech rate and mid-clause pauses every month

Progress needs measuring, because learners misjudge it. A meta-analysis of 22 studies found that **speed, and how often a speaker pauses in the middle of a clause**, best predict how fluent listeners judge someone's speech to be ([Suzuki, Kormos & Uchihara 2021](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/152963)). Learners' ratings of their own speaking match external measures only moderately, with correlations of about .44–.55 ([Butler review](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/language-teaching/article/selfassessment-in-second-language-learning/00D73BD54F6227D484297EEAB8F51BC0); [Winke, Zhang & Pierce](https://cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/S0272263122000079)). A workable routine is to record two to three minutes once a month on the same prompt ("explain a system you built"), plus one new prompt of the same type. Count morae per second, and count silent pauses of a quarter-second or longer inside clauses. From a transcript, count errors per 100 words, correcting the transcript against the audio, because speech-to-text output can look more correct than the speech was ([LET Journal](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/letcj/35/0/35_15/_article/-char/en)). The fixed prompt tracks your rehearsed performance; the new one shows whether the skill carries over to new material. Counting morae, and treating te-forms, から and けど as clause boundaries, are reasonable adaptations to Japanese, but research has not yet validated them. Fluency usually improves first. If accuracy lags, add planned and untimed practice.

For an outside check, ALC's JSST is a 15-minute phone test of 10 questions, scored by three raters on five criteria including 待遇表現, for ¥7,150–8,250. ALC's FAQ says, however, that individual applications are currently suspended ([ALC JSST](https://www.alc-education.co.jp/business/jsst/); [ALC FAQ](https://www.alc-education.co.jp/business/jsst/faq.html)). The ACTFL OPIc can be taken remotely in Japanese, in 20–40 minutes ([LTI](https://www.languagetesting.com/oral-proficiency-interview-by-computer-opic)). Nothing shows that IT employers ask for either score, so mock interviews remain the real test. The horizon is long. The US Foreign Service Institute budgets about 2,200 class hours for Japanese to reach professional working proficiency ([Atlas & Boots](https://www.atlasandboots.com/foreign-service-institute-language-difficulty/)), and those hours include the speaking practice that JLPT study leaves out.

## Conclusion

This learner's real constraint is not the lack of Japanese people nearby. It is the lack of feedback on a narrow set of routines, and feedback is now cheap to buy. Hiring and offshore work run on a closed set of patterned routines, so a Vietnam-based engineer can make the interview and stand-up routines automatic long before reaching N2. Apply to N3-level Japan-facing developer and tester roles as soon as those routines hold up under a tutor's follow-up questions. The first Japanese-facing job is both the goal and the best training method, because it turns the rest of the climb to BrSE into daily practice with real consequences.

The evidence also shows how to split the work between people and machines. AI partners excel at volume and at removing the fear of being judged. But they are weakest exactly where Vietnamese speakers are weakest, in vowel length, pitch and appropriate keigo, so spend paid human attention there and use OJAD between lessons. Because the evidence comes mostly from short studies of learners of English, treat the plan as a hypothesis. If three months of recordings show speech rate stuck and mid-clause pauses not falling, the plan needs more repetition and more correction, not more input.
