## 1. Job paths open to a Vietnamese engineer with Japanese, the Japanese level each path asks for, and the salary premium

### Takeaway
In Vietnam, **JLPT N2 plus about 2 years of experience is the usual floor for Bridge SE (BrSE) and IT comtor roles.** Offshore developer and tester roles at Japan-facing companies often accept **N3**, because a comtor or BrSE handles the client. A few BrSE postings also accept N3, mostly fresher roles or "N2/N3" roles. Onsite work in Japan and client-facing work usually expect N2 or higher, plus real conversational ability, which is checked in the interview. Japanese brings a measurable pay premium: TopDev found postings that require Japanese or Korean pay **at least 38% above the market average (2023)**. Most of the gain comes from getting into the role (developer to BrSE or PM), not from the small monthly certificate allowances.

**E. Visa (Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services, 技術・人文知識・国際業務, "gijinkoku")**
- The basic route is a university degree or enough relevant work experience. One guide gives 10 years of experience for the Engineer or Humanities track and 3 years for International Services work such as translation or language teaching. [GUIDE/OFFICIAL] — [Migaku](https://migaku.com/blog/language-fun/japan-engineer-specialist-in-humanities-visa-requirements-explained); [Study in Japan (JASSO)](https://www.studyinjapan.go.jp/en/work-in-japan/employment/status.html)
- **IT exam route for Vietnamese candidates:**
  - Japan's IT engineer exams are written into a Ministry of Justice notice (告示). People who pass a mutually recognized foreign exam get a special case in the gijinkoku landing criteria, which stands in for the degree requirement.
  - Japan and Vietnam signed mutual recognition on 4 July 2002 (revised 18 Jan 2005 and 14 Feb 2012).
  - The exams in Vietnam are run by HITC (formerly VITEC). Asia-common ITPEC exams have been held in Vietnam since 2006.
  - [OFFICIAL] — [IPA mutual recognition](https://www.ipa.go.jp/shiken/asia/mutual-recognition/about.html); [IPA Vietnam page](https://www.ipa.go.jp/shiken/asia/mutual-recognition/vietnam.html); [MOJ notice page](https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/policies/bill/nyukan_hourei_h09.html)
- **2026 change:**
  - For applications from **15 April 2026**, gijinkoku applicants must prove language ability at **CEFR B2**. Accepted proofs include JLPT N2 or BJT 400+.
  - The rule covers applicants at Category 3–4 employers who **mainly do interpersonal work that uses language skills**.
  - Vialto says it generally does **not** apply to "Engineer" or "Specialist in Humanities" roles. Coto Academy says it applies only to jobs where Japanese is actually used.
  - Students moving directly from student status after studying in Japan are exempt.
  - Several administrative scriveners (行政書士) say the same proof is needed if the job uses another language such as English, and that it applies at the first renewal after a job change.
  - Sources disagree on the exact scope, and I could not verify the official ISA notice. [GUIDE/legal advisory] — [Fragomen](https://www.fragomen.com/insights/japan-tightened-documentation-requirement-and-introduction-of-language-requirement-for-certain-work-visas.html); [Vialto Partners](https://vialtopartners.com/regional-alerts/japan-immigration-immigration-reform-2026-2029-key-changes-and-what-they-mean-for-employers-and-individuals); [Coto Academy](https://cotoacademy.com/japan-require-jlpt-n2-working-visas/); [SoraNews24, 7 Apr 2026](https://soranews24.com/2026/04/07/japan-reportedly-adding-japanese-language-skill-requirement-to-most-common-foreigner-work-visa/); [solution-supporter.jp (行政書士)](https://solution-supporter.jp/gijinkoku-japanese-language-requirement/); [imsvisa.support](https://imsvisa.support/post-7368/); [fuji-visa.net](https://fuji-visa.net/gijinkoku-japanese-proficiency-2026/)

### Inferences
- **Realistic ladder for this learner (N4–N3 now):**
  1. With N3 and basic speaking: offshore developer or tester roles at Japan-facing companies (Fujitsu VN, FPT, NTT DATA VN and similar), where a comtor or BrSE talks to the client.
  2. With N2 and about 2 years of Japan-project experience: mainstream BrSE.
  3. With N2–N1 and very strong listening and speaking: comtor/interpreter.

  "N3 BrSE" postings exist but are few and mostly fresher roles.
- The certificate allowance is small: going from N3 to N2 adds about ₫1M per month at Reeracoen. The jump in role is far bigger: comtor ₫20–30M, BrSE ₫35–75M, PM ₫40–70M+. Billing rates point the same way: BrSE is billed about 48% above a programmer (¥590k vs ¥400k). So the real "Japanese premium" is **access to BrSE/PM roles**, and that access depends on proven speaking and client-handling skill, not on the JLPT pass alone.
- JLPT has no speaking section (see Q3), so "N2" in a posting works as a CV filter. Speaking ability is then judged in the interview.
- **For Japan:** by law, the engineer visa does not need Japanese, and per Vialto the April 2026 B2 rule mostly targets language-based interpersonal work. So Japanese requirements for engineers in Japan are mostly set by employers. A comtor/interpreter-type job in Japan probably would fall under the B2 proof rule at smaller (Category 3–4) employers. This is an inference; verify against the ISA text.
- A Vietnamese engineer without a relevant degree could use the mutually recognized Vietnamese IT exam (ITPEC/HITC) as the degree substitute. Check which exam levels are listed in the MOJ notice.

### Gaps
- I found no 2025–2026 survey that isolates the Japanese-language pay premium in Vietnam. The 38% figure is from TopDev 2023. TopDev's 2025–2026 report and ITviec's Japanese breakdown were not found.
- Postings rarely state the *speaking* level they expect beyond "giao tiếp tốt" (good communication). No standard exists.
- I could not verify the official ISA wording for the April 2026 language rule (covered roles, accepted tests). I also could not confirm which Vietnamese exam levels the MOJ IT notice lists.
- I found no reliable figures for onsite pay in Japan for Vietnamese BrSEs (only scattered postings).

## 2. Market context: Vietnam–Japan IT offshoring, Japan's IT talent shortage, demand for Japanese-speaking engineers, and how much of Japan's tech market works in English only

### Takeaway
Vietnam is Japan's number-one offshore development destination: a bit over 40% ("4割強") of offshore orders in the 2025 white paper. Vietnamese firms are growing fast in Japan; FPT's Japan revenue grew 25–36% a year in 2024–2025. METI projects a large shortfall of IT and digital workers: up to about 790k IT workers by 2030 (2019 study), and about 3.4M "AI/robot-utilization" workers by 2040 (2026 revision). In the English-friendly niche that TokyoDev and Japan Dev cover, about half of international developers rarely or never use Japanese at work. That niche favors senior engineers at foreign-headquartered firms. At Japanese-headquartered firms, in SI and offshore work, and for juniors, Japanese stays central.

## 3. Hiring: how Japanese ability is evaluated, the common interview questions, what interviewers expect, and opening and closing phrases

### Takeaway
Employers screen on the JLPT level in the CV, but judge speaking in a **Japanese-language interview**, because **JLPT tests only reading and listening**. Interviewers check Japanese through the self-introduction and career explanation, job-related questions, sometimes role-play, and keigo and manners. A Japanese mid-career interview usually runs 自己紹介 (self-introduction) → 転職理由 (reason for changing jobs) → 志望動機 (motivation) → 自己PR (self-promotion) → 逆質問 (the candidate's own questions). The self-introduction should take about **1 minute (about 300 Japanese characters)**. Answers should be concise, specific, positive, linked to the company, and consistent with the CV.

### Inferences
- **What interviewers listen for:**
  - polite but natural keigo (です/ます forms plus a few set humble expressions such as 申します, 参りました, 携わってまいりました)
  - conclusion first (PREP: Point → Reason → Example → Point; standard Japanese business framing, but no PREP-specific source was retrieved)
  - answers that sound spontaneous rather than memorized: they survive follow-up 深掘り (probing) questions, which is why the Axxis advice is to embed episodes
  - asking for clarification politely instead of guessing
- Based on the method guides above, a Vietnam-based candidate should prepare five speakable blocks:
  1. a 1-minute 自己紹介
  2. a 2–3-minute explanation of a project (domain, team size, your phase or role, tech, result)
  3. 一番苦労したこと (hardest experience) told as problem → action → result
  4. 志望動機 and 入社後にやりたいこと (what you want to do after joining)
  5. two or three 逆質問
- **Illustrative phrases.** These were composed by the researcher in standard business Japanese and are NOT from a cited source. Have a teacher or native speaker check them.

  - Project explanation: 「前職では、日本のお客様向けの在庫管理システムの開発に参加しました。チームは8名で、私は詳細設計から単体テストまでを担当しました。」 (*Zenshoku de wa, Nihon no o-kyakusama-muke no zaiko kanri shisutemu no kaihatsu ni sanka shimashita. Chīmu wa hachi-mei de, watashi wa shōsai sekkei kara tantai tesuto made o tantō shimashita.*)

  - If you don't understand: 「申し訳ございません。もう一度ご質問をお聞きしてもよろしいでしょうか。」 (*Mōshiwake gozaimasen. Mō ichido go-shitsumon o o-kiki shite mo yoroshii deshō ka.*)
  - 逆質問: 「入社までに勉強しておいたほうがよい技術や業務知識はありますか。」 (*Nyūsha made ni benkyō shite oita hō ga yoi gijutsu ya gyōmu chishiki wa arimasu ka.*)
- Other questions named in the brief (長所・短所 strengths and weaknesses, 退職理由 reason for leaving, 入社後にやりたいこと what you want to do after joining) are standard in Japanese interviews, but this search did not surface a source listing them specifically for engineers. Treat them as expected rather than verified.

## 4. Workplace speaking situations, expected phrases and style, problems Japanese clients report, and habits successful BrSEs recommend

### Takeaway
Work speaking for BrSEs and Japanese-speaking engineers centers on a few situations:
- **報連相 (hōrensō: report, inform, consult)**: early, conclusion first, facts kept separate from guesses, a countermeasure or a request for a decision attached
- **spec clarification** through Q&A sheets and recognition alignment (認識合わせ): narrowed questions with options, 復唱 (repeating back), and stating exactly what the other side should check
- **incident first reports (第一報)** with four facts, sent before the cause is known
- **V-model phase vocabulary**

The problems Japanese clients report most often are spec misunderstandings that arise through translation, language silos, literal translation that misses implied requests, and poor visibility of progress.

### Cited Findings
**報連相 and progress reporting**
- A Viblo BrSE writes that each client company has its own way of working, but it mostly comes down to hōrensō. Use keigo flexibly and adapt to each client's style and communication habits. [ANECDOTAL] — [Viblo: communicating with Japanese clients](https://viblo.asia/p/giao-tiep-voi-nguoi-nhat-va-kinh-nghiem-khi-giao-tiep-voi-khach-hang-nhat-trong-du-an-dWrvwdOVRw38)
- Definitions: 報告 (report) shares results and progress, 連絡 (inform) shares facts with the people involved, 相談 (consult) asks for a decision. [GUIDE] — [Money Forward](https://biz.moneyforward.com/work-efficiency/basic/28242/)
- Reporting a delay:
  - Report as soon as you know; the earlier, the more options there are (adding people, cutting scope).
  - State the conclusion first: the subject, what is late, and the effect on the deadline.
  - Separate facts ("仕様変更が2件あった", there were two spec changes) from interpretation, and mark guesses with 「〜と考えられます」 (*… to kangaeraremasu*, "it is thought that").
  - [GUIDE] — [atsoho](https://atsoho.com/blog/progress-delays-report)
- Attach proposed next steps and the points that need the manager's decision. [GUIDE] — [Asana JP](https://asana.com/ja/resources/what-is-business-progress-report)
- An urgent progress-check email lists three items: 現在の状況 (current status), 課題・懸念点 (issues and concerns), 完了見込み (expected completion). [GUIDE] — [Money Forward PDF](https://biz.moneyforward.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/minibook_work-efficiency_11497.pdf)
- A former Mercari engineer with 10 years in Japan says a short summary of the issue plus a concrete plan is more useful than a detailed status list. He also discusses differences in directness and turn-taking. [ANECDOTAL/expert] — [TokyoDev, "Working and Communicating with Japanese Engineers"](https://www.tokyodev.com/articles/working-and-communicating-with-japanese-engineers)

**Spec confirmation, Q&A sheets (QA表) and 認識合わせ**
- A BrSE resolves misunderstandings caused by differences in language, culture and business practice. The work runs from explaining specs to writing spec and design documents. [GUIDE] — [doda](https://doda.jp/engineer/guide/qa/010.html)
- Q&A is a way of passing on the spec, alongside spec documents and reviews. The questions show whether the spec has been understood. [ACADEMIC] — [NAIST thesis](https://naist.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/8932/files/R007358.pdf)
- When the client answers questions late, the offshore side sits idle. The client should name who decides things (who checks acceptance, who reviews technically). Business flows, exception handling, screen items and approval rules don't reach an overseas team unless they are written down. [GUIDE] — [shopowner-support](https://www.shopowner-support.net/?p=264878); [shopowner-support](https://www.shopowner-support.net/?p=265082)
- Swapping Japanese for another language doesn't carry over the business assumptions or decision criteria behind a requirement. The spec has to be made complete, including its purpose and its users. If you notice something ambiguous but ask a rough question, alignment stalls. Narrow the point and offer options so it is easy to answer. The search extract could not confirm which of these pages each point came from. [GUIDE] — [shopowner-support](https://www.shopowner-support.net/?p=265082) / [kotora](https://www.kotora.jp/c/?p=30673)
- 「復唱は、相手の言ったことを繰り返す確認方法です」: 復唱 means repeating back what the other person said, to confirm both sides understood the same thing. [GUIDE: tech-company blog] — [Classmethod](https://dev.classmethod.jp/articles/confirmation-talk-and-repeat/)
- Phrases for spec and recognition checks: 「認識に相違がないかご確認ください」 (*ninshiki ni sōi ga nai ka go-kakunin kudasai*, "please check that our understanding matches"); 「仕様をご確認のうえ、ご意見をいただけますと幸いです」 (*shiyō o go-kakunin no ue, go-iken o itadakemasu to saiwai desu*, "I would be grateful for your comments after you check the spec"). The softer 「ご確認いただけますでしょうか」 (*go-kakunin itadakemasu deshō ka*) replaces 「ご確認ください」. A good request names what to check (document version), the scope (whole document or changed parts only), the deadline and how to reply. A bare 「資料をご確認ください」 is ambiguous: content check, approval, or typo check? [GUIDE] — [atsoho](https://atsoho.com/blog/check-requests-email-external); [Luft](https://www.luft.co.jp/media/confirmation-rephrasing/); [EngageLab](https://www.engagelab.com/ja_JP/blog/confirmation-emails); [DIME](https://dime.jp/genre/1905713/)
- Prepare well before a "hearing" (requirements interview) with the client and learn the client's industry. Communication quality, and the information you collect, are higher when you do. [ANECDOTAL] — [Viblo hearing series, part 1](https://viblo.asia/p/for-brse-comunication-skills-communication-skill-dung-cho-khi-hearing-voi-khach-hang-part-1-Eb85oVWBl2G); [part 2](https://viblo.asia/p/for-brse-comunication-skills-communication-skill-dung-cho-khi-hearing-voi-khach-hang-part-2-RQqKLGV457z)

**Incident reports (障害報告)**
- Send the first report even if the cause is unknown, with the facts: when it started, which functions and users are affected, what is being done now, and when the next report will come. Waiting for the cause before sending a first report is the most common failure. For high-impact incidents, one guide gives a target of within 30 minutes. Leave guesses about the cause out. Put the apology and resolution at the end. The final report covers date and time, location, person in charge, details, cause, response and preventive measures.   The clearest documented cause of client pain is spec misunderstanding through translation, which these habits address.
- **Illustrative workplace phrases.** Composed by the researcher following the sourced guidance above; NOT verbatim from sources, so verify with a teacher.
  - 朝会 (daily stand-up): 「昨日はログイン画面の実装を完了しました。今日は単体テストを進める予定です。今のところ、問題はありません。」 (*Kinō wa roguin gamen no jissō o kanryō shimashita. Kyō wa tantai tesuto o susumeru yotei desu. Ima no tokoro, mondai wa arimasen.*)
  - 進捗報告, delay: 「〇〇機能の進捗についてご報告します。結論から申し上げますと、2日ほど遅れる見込みです。原因は仕様変更が2件あったためです。対策として、テストの順番を入れ替えることを考えておりますが、ご相談させていただけますか。」 (*… kinō no shinchoku ni tsuite go-hōkoku shimasu. Ketsuron kara mōshiagemasu to, futsuka hodo okureru mikomi desu. Gen'in wa shiyō henkō ga ni-ken atta tame desu. Taisaku to shite, tesuto no junban o irekaeru koto o kangaete orimasu ga, go-sōdan sasete itadakemasu ka.*)
  - 仕様確認 and 復唱: 「確認させてください。〜という理解で合っていますでしょうか。」 (*Kakunin sasete kudasai. … to iu rikai de atte imasu deshō ka.*) / 「念のため復唱します。〜ということですね。」 (*Nen no tame fukushō shimasu. … to iu koto desu ne.*)
  - Question with options: 「エラー時の表示について、A案（ダイアログ表示）とB案（画面上部にメッセージ表示）のどちらがよろしいでしょうか。」 (*Erā-ji no hyōji ni tsuite, A-an (daiarogu hyōji) to B-an (gamen jōbu ni messēji hyōji) no dochira ga yoroshii deshō ka.*)
  - 障害 第一報 (incident first report): 「障害の第一報です。本日10時5分頃から、ログイン機能でエラーが発生しており、全ユーザーがログインできない状態です。原因は現在調査中です。次回は11時にご報告いたします。」 (*Shōgai no daiippō desu. Honjitsu jūji go-fun goro kara, roguin kinō de erā ga hassei shite ori, zen-yūzā ga roguin dekinai jōtai desu. Gen'in wa genzai chōsa-chū desu. Jikai wa jūichi-ji ni go-hōkoku itashimasu.*)
  - 見積もり (estimate): 「この改修の工数は、テストを含めて5人日と見積もっています。前提として、API仕様は変わらないものとしています。」 (*Kono kaishū no kōsū wa, tesuto o fukumete go-ninnichi to mitsumotte imasu. Zentei to shite, API shiyō wa kawaranai mono to shite imasu.*)
  - Code review or design discussion: 「この部分は共通化したほうが保守しやすいと思いますが、いかがでしょうか。」 (*Kono bubun wa kyōtsūka shita hō ga hoshu shiyasui to omoimasu ga, ikaga deshō ka.*)
  - End of meeting and minutes: 「本日の決定事項と宿題を確認させてください。議事録は本日中に共有いたします。」 (*Honjitsu no kettei jikō to shukudai o kakunin sasete kudasai. Gijiroku wa honjitsu-chū ni kyōyū itashimasu.*)

## 6. What hiring managers and experienced Vietnamese BrSEs say separates "good enough" spoken Japanese for work from textbook Japanese

### Takeaway
"Good enough" means:
- understanding what the client really needs and passing it on accurately, rather than translating word for word
- confirmation habits (復唱, narrowed questions with options)
- conclusion-first, structured reporting
- flexible, appropriate keigo
- following meetings by ear

JLPT N2 is a filter, not proof: employers check speaking through interviews and role-play. Evidence is mostly guides and practitioner accounts; there is no systematic survey.

### Cited Findings
- "Language ability" for a BrSE means being able to talk with clients to understand what they actually need, not just translate. [ANECDOTAL] — [Viblo: BrSE language skills](https://viblo.asia/p/ky-nang-ve-ngon-ngu-can-co-cua-brse-xu-ly-thong-tin-va-truyen-dat-thong-tin-XL6lANxJ5ek); [Viblo: what it takes to become a BrSE](https://viblo.asia/p/de-tro-thanh-brse-can-nhung-yeu-to-gi-Do754NbJZM6)
- In client communication, the key is using keigo flexibly and adapting to each client's working style. [ANECDOTAL] — [Viblo](https://viblo.asia/p/giao-tiep-voi-nguoi-nhat-va-kinh-nghiem-khi-giao-tiep-voi-khach-hang-nhat-trong-du-an-dWrvwdOVRw38)
- Literal translation misses requests that sound like questions but are urgent instructions. [ANECDOTAL] — [Viblo: comtor](https://viblo.asia/p/mot-vai-kinh-nghiem-lam-nghe-comtor-maGK7q4Olj2)
- JLPT doesn't test speaking, and N1/N2 holders can still struggle with keigo and business email. ### Inferences
- **Textbook vs work Japanese:** textbook N3/N2 study builds vocabulary and grammar for reading and listening. Work Japanese is a smaller set of high-stakes spoken routines:
  - a self-introduction and project explanation that survive follow-up questions
  - stand-up updates
  - conclusion-first progress and delay reports
  - clarifying questions with options and 復唱
  - an incident first report
  - estimate statements with their assumptions (前提)
  - closing a meeting with decisions and action items

  On top of these routines come two skills: following fast meeting talk by ear, and recognizing indirect requests.
- **A practical "good enough" bar for this learner:**
  - for an offshore developer role (N3): clear stand-up and progress reports, and polite clarification requests
  - for BrSE (N2): running a requirements hearing and Q&A, reporting delays and incidents with correct structure and keigo, and turning ambiguous Japanese into a clear spec for the team

  This is an inference built from the role descriptions and guides above.
- Because a candidate in Vietnam has no daily chances to speak Japanese, rehearsing these fixed scenes (interview blocks, stand-up, delay report, incident first report, spec Q&A with 復唱) probably gives more hiring value per hour than general conversation. Methods for practicing are covered by other researchers.

### Gaps
- I found no systematic survey of Japanese hiring managers or clients that rates Vietnamese engineers' spoken Japanese, or that defines a pass bar for speaking beyond the JLPT level.
- I found no recent Japanese-language interviews with Vietnamese BrSEs on "what made the difference". The Vietnamese-language evidence is Viblo posts and forums, which are anecdotal.
- I could not fetch Mynavi IT Agent, Reeracoen and the TokyoDev survey pages directly, because those hosts were blocked in this session.
