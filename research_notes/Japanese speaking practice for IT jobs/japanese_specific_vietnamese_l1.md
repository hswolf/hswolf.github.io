## 1. Pronunciation and prosody difficulties of Vietnamese L1 learners, and training that helps

### Takeaway
Japanese research on Vietnamese learners keeps finding the same problems: (a) the special morae, long vowels above all, in both hearing and speaking; (b) Vietnamese syllable-timed rhythm carried into mora-timed Japanese; (c) pitch habits that come from Vietnamese tones (a high, flat delivery and sudden pitch jumps at the start of phrases); and (d) a few consonant confusions (ザ/ジャ/ヤ, ツ→チュ, ナ/ダ/ラ, devoiced す). The training studies are small. They favor teaching rhythm and accent first, marking pitch visually, slow equal-length mora practice, and shadowing.

### Cited Findings
**Special morae (長音・促音・撥音)**
- Earlier studies repeatedly report that the special morae are hard for Vietnamese learners and that long vowels are the hardest. Tanaka gives the background that Vietnamese is a tonal language with no syllable-length contrast. Her study tested pronunciation teaching that focuses on accent as a way to fix long/short vowels. Before training, one learner said 主食 with a wrong HHL pattern. (Peer-reviewed bulletin.) — [田中真由美「ベトナム人日本語学習者における長・短音の習得 アクセントを重視した音声指導の効果について」JAFLE Bulletin 26](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jaflebulletin/26/0/26_20/_pdf/-char/en)
- Perception experiment: the most common error was hearing a short vowel said on low pitch as a long vowel. Learners also heard some short vowels as geminates. — [VNU-ULIS Journal of Foreign Studies: ベトナム人学習者の日本語における長音・促音の知覚に関する問題](https://jfs.ulis.vnu.edu.vn/index.php/fs/article/view/11)
- Vietnamese students majoring in Japanese were most accurate on geminates, then short vowels, then long vowels (long vowels hardest). Words with a high-low (HL) accent were harder to tell apart than low-high (LH) words. — [VNU-ULIS JFS: 長音・促音の聞きとりに与える長音の位置・アクセントの影響](https://jfs.ulis.vnu.edu.vn/index.php/fs/article/view/1069)
- Lengthening (長音化) in speech was analyzed by vowel, mora count and accent. The study found that producing the wrong accent type goes together with lengthening. — [CiNii: ベトナム人日本語学習者の日本語の産出における長音化現象](https://cir.nii.ac.jp/crid/1390572174440534912)
- Matsuda, Yoshida and Kanamura (Phonetic Society of Japan 2018 poster) found these rhythm errors: long vowels cut short (乗車 じょうしゃ → じょしゃ), long and short swapped (きゅうじゅうばん → きゅじゅーばん), vowels dropped where two vowels meet (整理券 せいりけん → せりけん), and extra geminates inserted. — [PSJ 2018 P09](https://conference.wdc-jp.com/psj/2018/contents/common/doc/P09.pdf)
- 杉本 (2005) had learners read aloud and found many lengthening and shortening errors, as cited in — [core.ac.uk paper](https://core.ac.uk/download/80041637.pdf). See also 杉本妙子「ベトナム語圏日本語学習者の発音の誤用と日越語音声の特徴について」 — [Studocu copy](https://www.studocu.com/vn/document/truong-dai-hoc-ngoai-ngu-dai-hoc-da-nang/ngoai-ngu-ii1-nhat/%E3%83%99%E3%83%88%E3%83%8A%E3%83%A0%E8%AA%9E%E5%9C%92%E6%97%A5%E6%9C%AC%E8%AA%9E%E5%AD%A6%E7%BF%92%E8%80%85%E3%81%AE%E7%99%BA%E9%9F%B3%E3%81%AE%E8%AA%A4%E7%94%A8%E3%81%A8%E6%97%A5%E8%B6%8A%E8%AA%9E%E9%9F%B3%E5%A3%B0%E3%81%AE%E7%89%B9%E5%BE%B4%E3%81%AB%E3%81%A4%E3%81%84%E3%81%A6/56809659)

**Rhythm (mora timing)**
- Matsuda, Yoshida and Kanamura (2019), 日本語音声コミュニケーション 7: 202–215, peer-reviewed: Vietnamese learners and native speakers read the same passage. Vowel length and vowel loudness vary from one vowel to the next much more in native speech, and the difference was significant (measured with PVI, a variability index). — [abstract, Hituzi Shobo](https://www.hituzi.co.jp/epublish/academic_journal/nhng_onsei/nihongoonsei007_nhg_06matsuda_ab.pdf)
- Vietnamese learners tend to fit Japanese into the syllable rhythm of Vietnamese ("音節リズムであるベトナム語のリズムにあてはめて日本語を発音しようとする傾向"). — [PSJ 2018 P09](https://conference.wdc-jp.com/psj/2018/contents/common/doc/P09.pdf)

**Pitch habits carried over from Vietnamese tones**
- Matsuda (teacher-training slides, 2016) lists these habits:
  - On 3–4-mora words with the accent on the last mora (尾高型), learners place the drop correctly but say the rest high and flat, which sounds unnatural.
  - Speech is high and flat overall.
  - Pitch jumps up or down sharply at the start of a sentence or phrase.
  - In words of 3 or more morae ending in う, pitch rises only on the mora just before it, which she links to Vietnamese checked syllables (入声).
  - Vietnamese has almost no intonation; Japanese does.
  
  — [松田真希子, ACRAS 研修資料 2016](http://www.acras.jp/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/55a243da409e42383ec120498cee00b7.pdf); [ACRAS report](http://www.acras.jp/?p=5980)
- In a study of how Vietnamese learners acquire pronunciation, their speech was rated less natural and gave a worse impression than native speech. It also showed frequent tension in the larynx, which makes the voice sound strained (苦しそう). — [ラーニングヒストリーから見たベトナム人日本語学習者の発音習得ストラテジーの研究 (academia.edu)](https://www.academia.edu/104755876/%E3%83%A9%E3%83%BC%E3%83%8B%E3%83%B3%E3%82%B0%E3%83%92%E3%82%B9%E3%83%88%E3%83%AA%E3%83%BC%E3%81%8B%E3%82%89%E8%A6%8B%E3%81%9F%E3%83%99%E3%83%88%E3%83%8A%E3%83%A0%E4%BA%BA%E6%97%A5%E6%9C%AC%E8%AA%9E%E5%AD%A6%E7%BF%92%E8%80%85%E3%81%AE%E7%99%BA%E9%9F%B3%E7%BF%92%E5%BE%97%E3%82%B9%E3%83%88%E3%83%A9%E3%83%86%E3%82%B8%E3%83%BC%E3%81%AE%E7%A0%94%E7%A9%B6)
- Anecdotal (non-academic blog): Vietnamese learners say katakana loanwords of 3 or more syllables flat, with a drop only at the end. — [note, Overseas University](https://note.com/oversea_u/n/nde876b14f8d5)

**Learners underrate the problem**
- In Matsuda's doctoral survey, learners rated listening as the hardest skill and did not think Japanese pronunciation was very difficult. — [松田真希子 博士論文要旨「ベトナム語母語話者のための日本語教育に関する総合的研究」](https://gensha.hit-u.ac.jp/archives/theses-archive/theses/89a.pdf)

**Consonants and devoicing**
- ヤ/ザ/ジャ: earlier studies (e.g., 川・中村 2005) report that Vietnamese learners mix up the ヤ, ザ and ジャ rows, and that ザ at the start of a word is often heard as ジャ. The usual explanation is that Vietnamese treats z-type and y-type onsets as one sound, said [z] in the North and [j] in the South. — [佐藤桐子, PSJ 2018 B3](https://conference.wdc-jp.com/psj/2018/contents/common/doc/B3.pdf)
- A teacher site explains the articulation: ザ is an alveolar affricate at the start of a word and a fricative in the middle, while ジャ is an alveolo-palatal affricate. Its drill is to start from [d] at the gum ridge and then move to [z]. — [kanjifumi.jp (teacher site)](https://www.kanjifumi.jp/seicho/14067/). The same site covers ヨ turning into ジョ — [podcast 52](https://www.kanjifumi.jp/podcast/052/)
- ツ → チュ: sources found in this search say learners can hear ツ but find it hard to say, and often replace it with チュ. — [杉本妙子 paper (Studocu copy)](https://www.studocu.com/vn/document/truong-dai-hoc-ngoai-ngu-dai-hoc-da-nang/ngoai-ngu-ii1-nhat/%E3%83%99%E3%83%88%E3%83%8A%E3%83%A0%E8%AA%9E%E5%9C%92%E6%97%A5%E6%9C%AC%E8%AA%9E%E5%AD%A6%E7%BF%92%E8%80%85%E3%81%AE%E7%99%BA%E9%9F%B3%E3%81%AE%E8%AA%A4%E7%94%A8%E3%81%A8%E6%97%A5%E8%B6%8A%E8%AA%9E%E9%9F%B3%E5%A3%B0%E3%81%AE%E7%89%B9%E5%BE%B4%E3%81%AB%E3%81%A4%E3%81%84%E3%81%A6/56809659); [teacher blog](https://ameblo.jp/vnlife2013/entry-11504184111.html)
- ナ/ダ/ラ: the Tokyo University of Foreign Studies (TUFS) Japanese pronunciation module gives ラ said as ナ (ライゲツ → ナイゲツ) and ダ/ラ mix-ups as typical errors. — [TUFS 言語モジュール 1.2.1 ラ行音](https://www.coelang.tufs.ac.jp/mt/ja/pmod/practical/01-02-01.php)
- A teacher site says that when learners say ナ the tongue tip touches the gum ridge too briefly, so it flaps and sounds like ラ. — [kanjifumi.jp](https://www.kanjifumi.jp/seicho/14065/). Vietnamese learners' ダ can sound like either ラ or ナ. — [kanjifumi.jp](https://www.kanjifumi.jp/seicho/15487/)
- Northern speakers reportedly tend to say the ラ row close to the ザ row, because Northern Vietnamese pronounces the letter r as [z]. These are teaching sources, and no controlled study turned up. — [TUFS module](https://www.coelang.tufs.ac.jp/mt/ja/pmod/practical/01-02-01.php); [Osaka Univ. Vietnamese phonetics handout](https://www1.lang.osaka-u.ac.jp/user/qingshui/vn13/listening.pdf)
- Vowel devoicing: a longitudinal study used awareness-raising with learners who could not produce the devoiced /u/ in です/ます. — [JLEM 22: ベトナム人日本語学習者における発音に対する意識化への試み](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jlem/22/1/22_KJ00009846945/_article/-char/ja/). The TUFS module has free lessons on producing devoiced vowels. — [TUFS 3.3.1](https://www.coelang.tufs.ac.jp/mt/ja/pmod/practical/03-03-01.php)

**Training that has been tried or recommended**
- Matsuda recommends getting learners used to saying morae slowly and at equal length, and notes this also works for Thai learners. — [ALCE pre-seminar slides](https://alce.jp/meeting/07/pre1pp.pdf); [ACRAS slides](http://www.acras.jp/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/55a243da409e42383ec120498cee00b7.pdf)
- Doan Le Hoai Anh (Nanzan Univ., PSJ 2019) asked whether Vietnamese tone marks could be used to write Japanese accent. Accent lines that show the height of each mora made correct pronunciation more likely, though this is suggestive only. — [PSJ 2019 B1](https://conference.wdc-jp.com/psj/2019/contents/common/doc/B1.pdf)
- Teacher advice: with Vietnamese learners, work on rhythm before consonants. — [kanjifumi.jp「ベトナム人への発音指導は子音よりリズム重視」](https://www.kanjifumi.jp/seicho/20655/)
- Shadowing cut errors on 促音, 長音 and 撥音 by more than 60%; the extract does not give the learners' first language. — [龍谷大学: シャドーイングの日本語音声教育における有効性](https://opac.ryukoku.ac.jp/iwjs0005opc/bdyview.do?bodyid=BD00003557&elmid=Body&fname=KJ00004859782.pdf). With Vietnamese learners, measuring prosody before and after shadowing training brought out problems such as speaking rate, and shadowing helped with many of them. — [academia.edu study](https://www.academia.edu/104755876/%E3%83%A9%E3%83%BC%E3%83%8B%E3%83%B3%E3%82%B0%E3%83%92%E3%82%B9%E3%83%88%E3%83%AA%E3%83%BC%E3%81%8B%E3%82%89%E8%A6%8B%E3%81%9F%E3%83%99%E3%83%88%E3%83%8A%E3%83%A0%E4%BA%BA%E6%97%A5%E6%9C%AC%E8%AA%9E%E5%AD%A6%E7%BF%92%E8%80%85%E3%81%AE%E7%99%BA%E9%9F%B3%E7%BF%92%E5%BE%97%E3%82%B9%E3%83%88%E3%83%A9%E3%83%86%E3%82%B8%E3%83%BC%E3%81%AE%E7%A0%94%E7%A9%B6)
- Teacher reference book written for Vietnamese learners: 金村久美・松田真希子『ベトナム人に日本語を教えるための発音ふしぎ大百科』(ひつじ書房, Aug 2020). — [books.or.jp](https://www.books.or.jp/book-details/9784894769199); [researchmap](https://researchmap.jp/read0103233/)

### Inferences
- Order of work for this learner: (1) mora timing and the special morae, since length changes which word you said; (2) phrase-level pitch, to fix the high-flat delivery and sudden jumps at phrase starts; (3) the few consonants tied to the learner's dialect: ザ/ジャ/ラ for Northern speakers, ザ→ヤ for Southern speakers.
- Long-vowel errors follow accent errors (the CiNii study, and Tanaka's accent-based training). So practicing length and pitch together, with visual pitch marks, is likely to work better than drilling length alone.
- Learners think their pronunciation is fine while it is rated less natural (Matsuda's survey vs. the academia.edu ratings). So recording yourself and comparing with a model, or getting outside feedback, is essential; self-assessment alone will miss the problems.

### Gaps
- No full texts were read, so effect sizes (for example Tanaka's results) and sample sizes are unknown.
- No empirical study turned up that links the n/l merger found in some Northern Vietnamese varieties to ナ/ラ confusion in Japanese. I know of that merger from general background; I did not confirm it in this research.
- No Vietnamese-specific study of the moraic nasal 撥音 turned up (for example ん before a vowel, as in 原因 or 恋愛, or a glottal stop inserted there).
- No Vietnamese-specific data turned up on mixing voiced and voiceless stops (か/が, た/だ, ぱ/ば).

## 2. Pitch accent and intonation: how much they matter, and self-study tools

### Takeaway
Japanese listeners' ratings of learner speech depend more on prosody (pitch above all) than on individual sounds, and accent errors make words harder to recognize. The habits listed for Vietnamese learners (flat delivery, sudden pitch jumps) are the same features native raters mark as sounding odd. Prosody is therefore worth training even at N3. Good tools exist: OJAD and its Suzuki-kun tutor (free), the NHK accent dictionary (paid app), and Dogen's course (paid).

### Cited Findings
- 佐藤友則 (1995), 世界の日本語教育 5: 139–154: in a listening experiment with synthesized speech, prosody affected native speakers' ratings of learner speech more than individual sounds did. Of the three prosodic elements (pitch, length, loudness), pitch mattered most, and the author recommends teaching natural pitch movement over time. — [core.ac.uk PDF (signed link, may expire)](https://pdf.core.ac.uk/corefilesystem/pdf/12356/234113099.pdf?se=2026-10-23T02%3A37%3A01Z&sp=r&sv=2026-06-06&sr=b&rscd=inline%3B+filename%3D%22234113099.pdf%22&rsct=application%2Fpdf&sig=VcVOwSvBIf0qkMl4URIQbWgeznuydcdObSYdM7RMQFk%3D). Later work cites it for this point, e.g. — [音声研究 23 (2019)](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/onseikenkyu/23/0/23_6/_pdf)
- 有賀照道 (Univ. of Tokyo, Linguistic Society of Japan meeting 168), semantic-priming study: words said with the wrong accent were recognized correctly less often, and accent errors got in the way of word recognition more than sound errors did (as the extract reports). — [LSJ handout](https://www.ls-japan.org/modules/documents/LSJpapers/meeting/168/handouts/b/B-3_168.pdf)
- 玉岡 et al., Chinese L1 learners: the ability to notice a wrong accent predicted listening scores; the ability to say accents correctly did not. — [tamaoka.org](https://tamaoka.org/scholarly/sadokuari/2016/143.pdf)
- 布村猛 et al.: native speakers rated learners' read-aloud speech on a 7-point scale and marked the spots that sounded odd (違和感). They marked word-accent errors, a flat tone (平坦な音調) and sudden pitch drops (ピッチの急降下). — [布村 2024 slides](https://www.tufs.ac.jp/ts/personal/ykawa/kaken2023/20240124_nunomura.pdf). The same team studied which learner and language factors affect word-accent acquisition. — [布村・阿部・川口 2022](https://www.tufs.ac.jp/ts/personal/ykawa/assets/images/nunomura_20220221.pdf)
- 稲田朋晃 (Nagoya Univ.): how learners group words into accent phrases and intonation phrases, including sentence-final patterns, strongly affects how Japanese their speech sounds, even when the errors do not change the meaning. — [稲田, Nagoya symposium](https://www.lang.nagoya-u.ac.jp/nichigen/menu7_folder/symposium/pdf/3/02.pdf)
- After instruction, the most improved items were phrase breaks, sentence-final intonation and geminates (one abstract in the PSJ 2018 session-A proceedings). — [PSJ 2018 A](https://conference.wdc-jp.com/psj/2018/contents/common/doc/A_ALL.pdf)
- The Univ. of Tokyo Minematsu lab measures how understandable non-native speech is by having native speakers shadow it. An IEICE 2019 report recorded Vietnamese learners reading aloud plus native speakers shadowing those recordings, growing from 6 learners in the earlier study to 60. — [IEICE 2019](https://ken.ieice.org/ken/paper/20190613x1nS/); [井上 et al., IPSJ SIG report](https://www.gavo.t.u-tokyo.ac.jp/~mine/CALL/ILS/Inoue_slp.pdf)

**Tools**
- OJAD (Online Japanese Accent Dictionary), Minematsu–Saito Lab, Univ. of Tokyo: free, with four functions. — [OJAD usage page](https://www.gavo.t.u-tokyo.ac.jp/ojad/pages/usage). Training slides by 峯松信明 and 中村則子: — [OJAD lecture (Seoul)](https://www.gavo.t.u-tokyo.ac.jp/~mine/OJAD/lecture/SEOUL/OJAD_Seoul_4up.pdf)
- Suzuki-kun (韻律読み上げチュータ スズキクン) is OJAD's prosody tutor. You type any Japanese text and it marks where the accent falls, draws the predicted pitch curve, and reads the text aloud with synthesized speech. It has beginner and advanced display modes. Its guesses are machine-made and sometimes wrong, and in the advanced display you can correct high and low morae by hand. — [teacher blog review](https://ameblo.jp/kumiakikomachi/entry-12663939713.html); [Japan Foundation teaching tip](https://www.jpf.go.jp/j/project/japanese/teach/tsushin/hint/201810.html)
- A free video lesson shows how to practice your own written text with Suzuki-kun. — [つたえるはつおん](https://www.japanese-pronunciation.com/jpn/movie/intonation2/). 中川千恵子 (Kokugakuin Univ.) wrote a step-by-step OJAD guide. — [やってみようOJAD PDF](https://www.japanese-pronunciation.com/assets/doc/practice/nakagawa-yattemiyo-ojad1.pdf)
- A review of computer-assisted (CALL) tools for teaching Japanese prosody appeared in the Journal of the Acoustical Society of Japan 72(4), 2016. — [J-STAGE](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jasj/72/4/72_213/_pdf)
- NHK日本語発音アクセント新辞典 (2016): community resource lists call it the most recent and most accurate pitch-accent dictionary. It is available in Monokakido's paid iOS dictionary app. — [xythh pitch-accent resources](https://xythh.github.io/resources); [k3zi pronunciation resources gist](https://gist.github.com/k3zi/a7b0493aca3f192bfc1e1bcd8216a719)
- Dogen's "Japanese Phonetics" (paid, on Patreon) has about 80 lessons: why phonetics matters, pitch-accent patterns, standard speaking style, and niche topics such as accent in names, numbers and titles. It is taught in English. — [Tofugu review](https://www.tofugu.com/reviews/dogen-japanese-phonetics/); [Patreon index](https://www.patreon.com/dogen/posts/japanese-index-16489306)

### Inferences
- Two findings point the same way. Prosody outweighs individual sounds in native ratings (Sato), and native raters mark flat tone and sudden pitch changes as odd (Nunomura), which are the habits listed for Vietnamese learners (Matsuda). So prosody training is likely one of the highest-value ways to improve how the learner comes across in interviews and meetings, even though no study tests workplace impressions directly.
- Word accent should not be ignored. Ariga found that accent errors hurt word recognition, so accent affects whether you are understood, not only how natural you sound. For a fixed set of IT-interview content, the cheapest route is: write the script (self-introduction, project explanation), run it through Suzuki-kun, shadow the synthesized model, record yourself, and compare.
- Tamaoka's result suggests that learning to *hear* accent errors also helps listening, so the time spent on accent pays off twice.

### Gaps
- I found no study of how pitch accent or intonation affects hiring decisions or how professional someone seems in Japanese workplaces.
- Praat, other pitch-visualizer apps and phone-app pitch features were not researched because the search budget ran out.
- 郡史郎's paper on how odd accent and intonation deviations sound to listeners — [Osaka Univ. PDF](http://www.lang.osaka-u.ac.jp/~caris/articles/%E3%82%A2%E3%82%AF%E3%82%BB%E3%83%B3%E3%83%88%E3%81%A8%E3%82%A4%E3%83%B3%E3%83%88%E3%83%8D%E3%83%BC%E3%82%B7%E3%83%A7%E3%83%B3%E3%81%AE%E9%80%B8%E8%84%B1%E3%81%AB%E5%AF%BE%E3%81%97%E3%81%A6%E6%84%9F%E3%81%98%E3%82%8B%E9%81%95%E5%92%8C%E6%84%9F%E3%81%AB%E3%81%A4%E3%81%84%E3%81%A6.pdf) — turned up in search, but I did not read it.

## 3. Shadowing for Japanese L2 learners: evidence, published materials, procedure

### Takeaway
Japanese-L2 studies report gains from shadowing in listening, repetition, test scores, the special morae, devoicing and intonation. Most of them have no control group or small samples, and the authors themselves warn against overclaiming. くろしお出版 publishes a job-interview shadowing book with a Vietnamese translation, plus a newer conversation series that also has Vietnamese translations. The usual procedure (listen → mumble → check meaning → read along → prosody shadowing → content shadowing) comes from the literature on English learners.

### Cited Findings
**Evidence**
- 迫田・リード・古本 (workshop handout, くろしお出版 2020) list three effects:
  - listening and repetition ability grow (from the repetition);
  - proficiency-test scores rise (from processing speech in real time);
  - pronunciation gets corrected and fluency grows (from natural input).

  They report that 迫田・松見 (2005) found shadowing may build practical speaking ability in learners studying outside Japan, but because there was no control group the effect cannot be firmly attributed to shadowing. — [迫田久美子 workshop PDF](https://www.9640.jp/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/2020shadowingWS_sakoda.pdf)
- 迫田久美子・古本裕美 (eds.)『日本語教師のためのシャドーイング指導』(くろしお出版, Dec 2019) is an introduction to teaching, assessing and using shadowing, with examples from other countries. — [CiNii Books](https://ci.nii.ac.jp/ncid/BB29435287); [くろしお event page](https://www.9640.jp/events/5353/)
- 戸田貴子 (Waseda): a 2007 paper asked "Does shadowing practice contribute to the ultimate attainment in L2 pronunciation?" A 2011 paper (戸田・大久保) reports that adults who started Japanese after the critical period can still reach native-level pronunciation, that these high achievers' comments had things in common, and reportedly that they used methods such as shadowing heavily. A 2015 presentation looked at internet-based pronunciation checks for Chinese learners. — [戸田研究室 実践・研究](https://gsjal.jp/toda/jissen.html); [経歴](https://gsjal.jp/toda/keireki.html)
- 田辺 (Gifu Univ., 2014): in class presentations and speeches, long vowels, vowel devoicing and intonation improved over time. The author warns the effect cannot be overstated because students also simply got used to the task, and that shadowing is mentally demanding, so level and material need care. — [岐阜大学留学生センター紀要 2014](https://www.glocal.gifu-u.ac.jp/docs/2014_tanabe.pdf)
- 望月通子 (Kansai Univ.): shadowing helps pronunciation, prosody (pitch, stress, rhythm) and listening, and listening gains show up within a short period. — [関西大学 紀要](https://www.kansai-u.ac.jp/fl/publication/pdf_aids/29/1-4_mochizuki.pdf)
- 龍谷大学 study: errors on 促音, 長音 and 撥音 fell by more than 60%. — [Ryukoku PDF](https://opac.ryukoku.ac.jp/iwjs0005opc/bdyview.do?bodyid=BD00003557&elmid=Body&fname=KJ00004859782.pdf)
- Studies of listening gains from shadowing for learners in China — [明海日本語 17 (2012)](http://urayasu.meikai.ac.jp/japanese/meikainihongo/17/kyo.pdf) — and for learners studying Japanese as a second vs. third language — [JLEM 29](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jlem/29/1/29_26/_pdf).
- Vietnamese-specific: shadowing training helped with many of the prosodic problems found, such as speaking rate (small study). — [academia.edu study](https://www.academia.edu/104755876/%E3%83%A9%E3%83%BC%E3%83%8B%E3%83%B3%E3%82%B0%E3%83%92%E3%82%B9%E3%83%88%E3%83%AA%E3%83%BC%E3%81%8B%E3%82%89%E8%A6%8B%E3%81%9F%E3%83%99%E3%83%88%E3%83%8A%E3%83%A0%E4%BA%BA%E6%97%A5%E6%9C%AC%E8%AA%9E%E5%AD%A6%E7%BF%92%E8%80%85%E3%81%AE%E7%99%BA%E9%9F%B3%E7%BF%92%E5%BE%97%E3%82%B9%E3%83%88%E3%83%A9%E3%83%86%E3%82%B8%E3%83%BC%E3%81%AE%E7%A0%94%E7%A9%B6). Native-speaker shadowing has also been used to *measure* how understandable Vietnamese learners' speech is. — [IEICE 2019](https://ken.ieice.org/ken/paper/20190613x1nS/)

**Published materials**
- 『シャドーイング 日本語を話そう！ 就職・アルバイト・進学面接編』 by 斎藤仁志, 深澤道子, 酒井理恵子 and 中村雅子 (くろしお出版) covers job, part-time-job and school-admission interviews plus the small talk before an interview. It drills the reasons for applying (志望動機) in particular and adds interview tips and stock phrases. The 2016 original came with 2 CDs. The 2023 audio-download edition keeps the same content and comes in an Indonesian/Thai/**Vietnamese** translation version (ISBN 9784874249468) and an English/Chinese/Korean version. — [版元ドットコム (Vietnamese-translation DL edition)](https://www.hanmoto.com/bd/isbn/9784874249468); [凡人社 (EN/ZH/KO edition)](https://www.bonjinsha.com/item/487424947); [2016 edition](https://www.maruzenjunkudo.co.jp/products/9784874246771). Series page: — [くろしお出版 シリーズ検索](https://www.9640.jp/nihongo/ja/search/?search=7&key=%3C%E3%82%B7%E3%83%A3%E3%83%89%E3%83%BC%E3%82%A4%E3%83%B3%E3%82%B0%E3%80%80%E6%97%A5%E6%9C%AC%E8%AA%9E%E3%82%92%E8%A9%B1%E3%81%9D%E3%81%86%3E)
- 『シャドーイング もっと話せる日本語』 (supervised by 迫田久美子, written and edited by 古本裕美, くろしお出版) aims at conversation one step up: from short to long conversations, and adjusting speech to the listener. The 初〜中級編 comes with English, Chinese and **Vietnamese** translations (ISBN 9784874249529). — [楽天ブックス](https://books.rakuten.co.jp/rb/17553227/). There is also a 中〜上級編 (ISBN 9784874249802). — [版元ドットコム](https://www.hanmoto.com/bd/isbn/9784874249802)
- 戸田貴子『シャドーイングで日本語発音レッスン』(スリーエーネットワーク, 2012): each lesson has a text to shadow, pronunciation points, exercises and a check/notes page, and takes 10–15 minutes. — [Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/en/book/show/41459083); 2009 teacher seminar on shadowing for smoother pronunciation — [日本台湾交流協会](https://www.koryu.or.jp/business/japanese/training/2009/detail17/)

**Procedure (mostly from the English-L2 literature)**
- 玉井健 lists six steps: listening, mumbling, checking the meaning with the text, reading along in sync with the audio, prosody shadowing and content shadowing. — [英語シャドーイング超入門 book page](https://eigovis.net/books/shadowing-entry.htm)
- Prosody shadowing means copying the sounds, rhythm and intonation exactly (beginner to intermediate). Content shadowing means shadowing while following the meaning (intermediate to advanced). Mumbling means murmuring along quietly without looking at the text. — [englead.jp](https://englead.jp/column/ng-shadowing/); [eigovis.net](https://eigovis.net/books/shadowing-entry.htm)
- Use material you almost fully understand. If you cannot keep up even when mumbling, the material is too hard for you. — [alohaenglish.jp](https://www.alohaenglish.jp/how-to-shadowing/); [useless-blog.com](https://useless-blog.com/contents-shadowing-effective-ways/)

### Inferences
- For this learner the most useful published book is the interview edition with the Vietnamese translation (job interviews, 志望動機). 『もっと話せる日本語 初〜中級編』 (also with Vietnamese) fits the N4–N3 stage, before moving up to the 中〜上級編.
- Shadowing is mentally demanding (Tanabe), so at N3 the learner should shadow transcripts already understood and should record and compare against the model. Toda's internet pronunciation checks and OJAD's visual pitch both support that comparison.
- No published shadowing book is aimed at IT work. A learner can make one: write project-explanation scripts, get model audio from Suzuki-kun or a native speaker, then shadow them.

### Gaps
- 門田修平's (Kadota) research was not retrieved. His work is mostly on English L2 and would add theory (the phonological loop, subvocal rehearsal).
- No business- or IT-specific edition of the くろしお series turned up. Whether the older 初〜中級 and 中〜上級 volumes of 『シャドーイング 日本語を話そう』 are still in print was not checked.
- The exact steps that the くろしお Japanese books recommend could not be confirmed, because their full texts were not available.

## 4. The Sino-Vietnamese (Hán Việt) advantage: sound correspondences, evidence and false friends

### Takeaway
About half of the two-kanji words in JLPT lists, and about a quarter of all JLPT vocabulary, match or resemble Hán Việt words. The advantage starts at intermediate level and is largest for formal, written and technical vocabulary, which is the vocabulary of IT work. Hán Việt keeps the Middle Chinese word endings, so they predict Japanese on'yomi endings fairly regularly. Most importantly, a final -ng or -nh predicts a Japanese long vowel, which directly helps with the long-vowel weakness from question 1. Meanings sometimes differ and word order sometimes flips, so false friends need checking.

### Cited Findings
**Evidence of benefit**
- 松田真希子「ベトナム語母語話者にとって漢越語知識は日本語学習にどの程度有利に働くか—日越漢字語の一致度に基づく分析」, 世界の日本語教育 18 (sources give 2008):
  - in JLPT vocabulary, about 50% of two-kanji words and about 25% of all words match or are similar to Hán Việt words;
  - the matching words cluster in late-intermediate to advanced written vocabulary, so beginners gain little;
  - the benefit shows from intermediate level onward and is likely large for academic and technical terms.
  
  — [researchmap paper page](https://researchmap.jp/read0103233/published_papers/1795870)
- Matsuda's experiments conclude that teaching kanji and vocabulary through Hán Việt should start in late beginner level, once learners know some kanji, not in early beginner. — [researchmap](https://researchmap.jp/read0103233/published_papers/1795870); [doctoral thesis summary](https://gensha.hit-u.ac.jp/archives/theses-archive/theses/89a.pdf); [日振協 workshop slides 2014](https://www.nisshinkyo.org/news/pdf/20140819yb.pdf)
- ホアーン ティ ラン フォン & 玉岡 (Nagoya Univ., 2021) studied how sound similarity and word frequency affect Vietnamese learners' translation of two-kanji words. Early beginners hardly use Hán Việt. Intermediate learners use it to guess the meaning of kanji words that map one-to-one, which speeds up vocabulary and kanji learning. — [小出記念 research report PDF](https://koidekinen.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/2021_hoangtamaoka.pdf)
- In a small qualitative study of upper-intermediate learners (care-worker exam context), more than half said they use Hán Việt to work out on'yomi or meanings, and some said they do not. — [大阪大学 repository](https://ir.library.osaka-u.ac.jp/repo/ouka/all/87567/ffle_05_055.pdf)
- A Kyushu University repository paper suggests that knowing the Hán Việt–on'yomi correspondences may speed up guessing kanji words and learning vocabulary. — [Kyushu Univ. repository](https://api.lib.kyushu-u.ac.jp/opac_download_md/4784713/isgs0072.pdf)
- 上本洋平 (Waseda) studied how aware low-experience Vietnamese learners are of Hán Việt words, by country of residence (details not read). — [researchmap attachment](https://researchmap.jp/uemotoyohei/published_papers/49495915/attachment_file.pdf)
- A Vietnamese-life media site says 60–70% of Vietnamese words are Hán Việt. — [VIETJO](https://www.viet-jo.com/howto/vietnamese-language/127)

**Sound correspondences**
- Hán Việt keeps all the Middle Chinese contrasts between nasal and stop endings. Middle Chinese -ng appears as -ng, or -nh after front vowels; -k appears as -c, or -ch after front vowels. In Japanese go-on and kan-on readings, Middle Chinese -ng became a long vowel: almost all such characters end in ō, yō, ē, ū or yū. — [Wikipedia: Sino-Xenic vocabularies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Xenic_pronunciations)
- Stop endings, Middle Chinese → Hán Việt → Japanese on'yomi:

  | Middle Chinese | Hán Việt | Japanese go-on | Japanese kan-on |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | -p | -p | ɸu > u | ɸu > u |
  | -t | -t | chi (from ti) | tsu (from tu) |
  | -k | -c / -ch | ku / ki | ku / ki |

  — [Wikipedia: Sino-Xenic vocabularies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Xenic_pronunciations); [Wikipedia: Sino-Japanese vocabulary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Japanese_vocabulary)
- Vietnamese learner sites give rule lists and say they are rough guides with many exceptions; check with a dictionary. Examples:
  - initial NG- → g (NGŨ 五 → go, NGOẠI 外 → gai, NGỮ 語 → go, NGÔN 言 → gen);
  - rhymes ƯƠNG / ANG / ÔNG (also ONG, ĂNG, ÂNG) → -ou;
  - final -c → -ku;
  - initial C → k (高 CAO → コウ, 歌 CA → カ).

  — [Jasa](https://jasa.edu.vn/?chitiet=757); [tiengnhatmoingay "12 quy tắc"](https://tiengnhatmoingay.com/chuyen-tu-am-han-viet-sang-am-on/); [kosei.vn](https://kosei.vn/quy-tac-chuyen-am-han-tu-tieng-nhat)
- Japanese-language guides say that final consonants in Hán Việt correspond to on'yomi endings ン, ウ/イ, ク/キ and ツ/チ. Some compounds have reversed word order or a shifted meaning, and some Japanese words read with kun'yomi have an on-style reading in Vietnamese. — [langhacks](https://langhacks.com/vietnamese-han-viet/); [note (tatablog)](https://note.com/tatablog/n/n571f2c8bdabd)

**False friends**
- bác sĩ 博士 means "physician" in Vietnamese, while Japanese 博士 means a doctorate; the Vietnamese for a PhD is tiến sĩ 進士. giám đốc 監督 means a company director, which does not match Japanese 監督. Japanese Wikipedia has a table of these same-form, different-meaning words. — [Wikipedia 漢越語](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E6%BC%A2%E8%B6%8A%E8%AA%9E)
- học sinh 学生 means a school pupil, while a university student is sinh viên 生員, a word that once meant an examination candidate. This matters in self-introductions. Also: mỹ phẩm 美品 means "cosmetics" (Japanese 美品 means an item in mint condition); diễn xuất 演出 means "acting", while the film director is đạo diễn 導演; thủ đoạn 手段 is used only negatively ("scheme"); tác phong 作風 means a person's manner or attitude. — [langhacks](https://langhacks.com/vietnamese-han-viet/); [vietomato 漢越強化シリーズ](https://vietomato.com/han-viet-thu); [戸惑いの漢越語 (blog)](https://akiramerunabetonamugo.wordpress.com/2020/03/30/%E6%88%B8%E6%83%91%E3%81%84%E3%81%AE%E6%BC%A2%E8%B6%8A%E8%AA%9E/)
- 勉強 in Hán Việt is miễn cưỡng, "reluctantly, forced". The Japanese meaning "study" is Japan's own development, spread from early Meiji texts. — [Từ điển Hán Nôm: 勉強](https://hvdic.thivien.net/whv/%E5%8B%89%E5%BC%B7); [Diễn Đàn Khai Phóng](https://diendankhaiphong.org/tai-sao-nguoi-nhat-ban-dung-tu-mien-cuong-voi-y-nghia-la-hoc/)
- One Hán Việt syllable can stand for different characters, e.g. công is 公 in công viên 公園 but 工 in công nghiệp 工業. — [vietomato 漢越強化シリーズ](https://vietomato.com/han-viet-cach/)

### Inferences
- Use for speaking: the Hán Việt mainly helps the learner *recall and guess* the many formal 漢語 in IT talk. For speaking, the learner must also turn it into the right on'yomi, with the right length and accent, in real time. The -ng/-nh → long vowel rule is a direct fix for Vietnamese long-vowel errors:
  - 環境 hoàn cảnh → かんきょう: -nh gives a long vowel;
  - 報告 báo cáo → ほうこく;
  - 管理 quản lý → かんり;
  - 確認 xác nhận → かくにん: -c gives -ku;
  - 設計 thiết kế → せっけい: -t surfaces as っ before k, and kế becomes the long けい.
- Matsuda found the match rate is highest in late-intermediate to advanced written vocabulary. So the advantage should grow just as the learner moves from N3 to N2/N1 and into specialized IT vocabulary. A practical drill is a list of core IT 漢語 grouped by sound-correspondence pattern, said aloud with OJAD pitch.
- Hán Việt tones say nothing about Japanese pitch accent. The learner should look up each word's accent rather than carrying the Vietnamese tone over.

### Gaps
- No controlled study turned up on training *spoken* vocabulary with Hán Việt; the studies cover recognition, translation and kanji learning.
- Possible false friends in IT vocabulary, from my own background knowledge and not confirmed in this research (check in a Vietnamese–Japanese dictionary):
  - 環境: hoàn cảnh usually means "circumstances"; "environment" is usually môi trường;
  - 試験: thí nghiệm means "experiment";
  - 運用: vận dụng means "to apply (knowledge)", not "operate a system";
  - 障害: chướng ngại means "obstacle", not "system failure".
- Other Japanese/Chinese false friends turned up (阶段 vs 階段, 迷惑) — [dokochina](https://dokochina.com/aitama/47.htm) — but I could not confirm that they work the same way in Vietnamese.

## 5. Features of spoken Japanese to master, and common speaking errors of Vietnamese learners

### Takeaway
The evidence I could reach is thin and mostly about other first-language groups or non-academic. Too few or badly pitched あいづち make learners seem not to be listening. Keigo is a separate system that Vietnamese politeness (ạ, kinship pronouns) does not prepare learners for. Requests are the hardest speech act for intermediate learners. Official guidance (敬語の指針, 2007) gives the five-way keigo system and the conditions for 「させていただく」. I could not search fillers, ellipsis, sentence-final particles or polite/casual switching before the budget ran out.

### Cited Findings
**あいづち (backchannelling)**
- Indonesian learners who gave few あいづち were taken by native speakers as not listening or not understanding. The authors put this down to unfamiliarity with the Japanese co-constructed conversation style (共話). — [大阪大学: 日本語とインドネシア語のあいづちの使用に関する対照研究](https://ir.library.osaka-u.ac.jp/repo/ouka/all/54503/sjlc25_133.pdf)
- Chinese learners who repeated 「そうか」 with a certain intonation sounded odd or were misunderstood. The same あいづち form carries different feelings depending on intonation and accent, so it can send an unintended message. — [名古屋大学 symposium paper on そうか/そっかー](https://www.lang.nagoya-u.ac.jp/bugai/kokugen/nichigen/menu7_folder/symposium/pdf/2/10.PDF)
- A Japanese-language note on *Vietnamese* conversation says the basic Vietnamese backchannel is a はい-type word, and that repeating そうですか-type phrases sounds odd to Vietnamese listeners (anecdotal). This suggests Vietnamese backchannel habits are sparser than Japanese ones. — [note, 田畑トマト](https://note.com/vietomato/n/ne6daf7640906)

**Keigo and politeness**
- Vietnamese honorifics are much less developed than Japanese keigo. Politeness is shown mainly through terms of address and word choice, and the simplest polite marker is a sentence-final ạ. — [vietomato vol.406](https://vietomato.com/cach-noi-kinh-ngu)
- Recruiting-company columns (no data behind them) say Vietnamese learners find keigo especially hard, and that Vietnamese say yes and no clearly and find indirect expressions hard. — [kedomo](https://career.kedomo.com/column/language_difference_vn/); [アイプランニング](https://aiplanning-company.com/wrok/post-1194/)
- 文化審議会答申『敬語の指針』 (2 Feb 2007) splits keigo into five types: 尊敬語, 謙譲語I, 謙譲語II (丁重語), 丁寧語 and 美化語. 謙譲語II describes your own side's actions politely to the listener (参る, 申す, いたす, おる, 拙著, 小社). いただく is 謙譲語I and also carries the sense of receiving a favor. — [敬語の指針 (文化庁 PDF)](https://www.bunka.go.jp/seisaku/bunkashingikai/kokugo/hokoku/pdf/keigo_tosin.pdf)
- 「させていただく」: guides that cite the 指針 say it fits when (1) you act with the permission of the listener or a third party and (2) you benefit from that. Otherwise use いたします or する, and avoid overusing it. — [Indeed Japan career advice](https://jp.indeed.com/career-advice/career-development/have-the-privilege-of-doing); [文賢マガジン](https://magazine.bun-ken.net/3378)

**Pronouns**
- Vietnamese chooses first- and second-person forms by age and relationship, mostly with kinship terms. — [TUFS Vietnamese grammar module](https://www.coelang.tufs.ac.jp/mt/vi/gmod/courses/c01/lesson08/step02/explanation/015.html). The neutral "you" for strangers is bạn. — [vietlovelog](https://vietlovelog.com/173/)
- Saying あなた to a superior's face in Japanese can sound distant or even rude. — [word-dictionary.jp](https://word-dictionary.jp/posts/6065/)

**Requests and refusals (pragmatics)**
- Nguyen Thi Nhu Y's Osaka Univ. dissertation compares how Japanese and Vietnamese show consideration in requests and proposes a teaching plan for Vietnamese learners. — [Osaka Univ. dissertation](https://ir.library.osaka-u.ac.jp/repo/ouka/all/67093/29349_Dissertation.pdf)
- Sumitomo Foundation report: refusal tasks (discourse completion tests) with 200 Japanese and 200 Vietnamese university students. When refusing, Vietnamese respondents weighed hierarchy (social power) more than closeness. — [住友財団 report](https://www.sumitomo.or.jp/pdf/jare/11_jare_seika/jare11-76.pdf)
- Intermediate–advanced learners rated requests the hardest of five speech acts (not only Vietnamese learners). — [JLEM 26](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jlem/26/1/26_48/_pdf)
- A 2018 review notes that research on refusals by learners of Japanese has centered on Chinese and Korean speakers, with Thai and Vietnamese learners only recently studied. — [宗甜甜 2018](https://gssc.dld.nihon-u.ac.jp/wp-content/uploads/journal/pdf19/19-207-218-Zong.pdf). Hedges in learners' refusals of invitations — [堀田・堀江 2012, 語用論研究 14](http://pragmatics.gr.jp/content/files/SIP_014/SIP_14_Hotta_Horie.pdf)

**Register is tested**
- ALC's JSST speaking test scores "待遇表現" (register and politeness) as one of five criteria. — [ALC JSST](https://www.alc-education.co.jp/business/jsst/)

### Inferences
- Likely trouble spots for this learner:
  - too few, or too uniform, あいづち: Vietnamese はい-type backchannel habits plus the Indonesian finding;
  - overusing あなた or explicit pronouns, because Vietnamese needs a pronoun in nearly every sentence;
  - stopping at です/ます (mapping Vietnamese ạ onto it) and not mastering humble forms for your own actions (申します, 参ります, いたします, おります). Those forms dominate workplace talk: self-introduction, reporting, phone calls;
  - requests and refusals that come out too direct, or misjudge hierarchy.
- 「させていただく」 is a known overuse trap. Its two conditions (permission, benefit) give a simple self-check.
- Because JSST scores register separately, a learner can get measurable feedback on keigo, not only on fluency.

### Gaps
- Not researched because the search budget ran out:
  - fillers (えーと, あのう, そうですね);
  - ellipsis and sentence-final particles (ね, よ, よね);
  - polite/casual speech-level shifting (スピーチレベルシフト);
  - クッション言葉 (恐れ入りますが, お手数ですが).
- I found no academic study cataloguing the conversation errors of *intermediate Vietnamese* learners. NINJAL's I-JAS learner corpus may include Vietnamese speakers' spoken data, but I did not confirm this.
- No Vietnamese-specific study of あいづち turned up.

## 7. Assessing spoken Japanese (the JLPT has no speaking section)

### Takeaway
Two standardized speaking tests can be taken from Vietnam: ACTFL OPI/OPIc through Language Testing International (remote, Japanese available) and ALC's JSST (by phone, 24 hours, from anywhere, though whether individuals can still apply is unclear). BJT, J.TEST, JFT-Basic and the JLPT have no speaking section, even though BJT and JFT-Basic have "conversation" sections. All of them except the speaking tests run test centers in Vietnam, BJT through Pearson VUE and JFT-Basic through Prometric. I found no data on how much IT employers weigh speaking scores; interviews remain the real test.

### Cited Findings
- **JLPT**: the sections are language knowledge, reading and listening; there is no speaking section. — [Wikipedia: JLPT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese-Language_Proficiency_Test)
- **ACTFL OPI / OPIc**:
  - The OPI is a speaking test usable for any language, with 10 levels from Novice to Superior. It is formally given by phone by an ACTFL-certified tester. The OPIc is a computer version that can be taken at any time. — [文化審議会「日本語教育の参照枠」二次報告 (2021)](https://www.bunka.go.jp/seisaku/bunkashingikai/kokugo/hokoku/pdf/92880801_01.pdf)
  - Language Testing International (LTI), ACTFL's exclusive licensee, describes the OPI as a live 15–30-minute interview. In the remote version you sign in to a remote-proctoring platform with a webcam, and the proctor has you phone the tester.
  - The OPIc takes 20–40 minutes online: an avatar asks the questions and an ACTFL-certified rater scores the recordings. Japanese is on the OPIc list. You need a computer, webcam, headset and microphone.
  
  — [LTI Japanese testing](https://www.languagetesting.com/japanese-testing); [LTI OPI](https://www.languagetesting.com/oral-proficiency-interview-opi); [LTI OPIc](https://www.languagetesting.com/oral-proficiency-interview-by-computer-opic)
  - Some organizations require the phone OPI and do not accept the OPIc. — [Univ. of Utah L2 TReC](https://l2trec.utah.edu/language-services/oral-proficiency-interview-tests.php). One university charges $80 per test, or $105 with remote proctoring, but that is its own fee, not LTI's public price. — [Univ. of Tennessee](https://gradschool.utk.edu/future-students/office-of-graduate-admissions/ita-testing-program/). Miami University lists Japanese among its on-demand OPIc languages. — [Miami Univ. ILRC](https://miamioh.edu/cas/centers-institutes/interactive-language-resource-center/testing/actfl.html)
  - Research on measuring Japanese speaking ability by computer with the OPIc: — [世良時子 (Seikei Univ.), LET 2018 slides](http://global8.or.jp/Ms.Sera_OPIcJ@2018LET.pdf)
- **JSST (ALC Japanese Standard Speaking Test)**:
  - Format: by phone, about 15 minutes, 10 questions from pre-recorded audio. You can take it 24 hours a day from inside or outside Japan, and you pay the call charges.
  - Scoring: 3 trained raters score five criteria (発話の形, 正確さ, 待遇表現, 内容, 総合タスク) on a 10-level scale.
  - It does not measure kanji or reading, so ALC suggests pairing it with the JLPT.
  
  — [ALC JSST](https://www.alc-education.co.jp/business/jsst/); [日本語ジャーナル 2022](https://shop.alc.co.jp/blogs/nihongo-journal/20220529-jsst); [ALC column on choosing tests](https://www.alc-education.co.jp/business/column/jp2207/)
  - Fees: ALC's business page lists ¥8,250 with a comment sheet and ¥7,150 for the score report only. Other pages show ¥8,800, and an older one ¥5,775. — [ALC JSST](https://www.alc-education.co.jp/business/jsst/); [JSST 個人受験 page](https://tsst.alc.co.jp/jsst/kojin_exam.html)
  - Conflict: ALC's FAQ says individual applications are currently suspended and companies can apply from one person upward, while the individual-application page still describes paying online by credit card. — [ALC FAQ](https://www.alc-education.co.jp/business/jsst/faq.html) vs. [JSST 個人受験](https://tsst.alc.co.jp/jsst/kojin_exam.html)
  - Use in practice: ALC presents it as a progress marker after hiring rather than a hiring tool. — [ALC column](https://www.alc-education.co.jp/business/column/jp2207/). Universities promote it to international students as something to show in job hunting, e.g. Gifu Univ.'s "就活時のPRに使えます" — [Gifu flyer](https://www.glocal.gifu-u.ac.jp/docs/JSSTflyer_jp.pdf); [Hokkaido Univ. JSST support 2024](https://fohred.synfoster.hokudai.ac.jp/old/en/20-2/1079-2/1085-2/17683-2.html); [Nagoya Univ. notice](https://keiji.i.nagoya-u.ac.jp/student_gakugai/?n=1104). ALC started taking applications in China. — [PR TIMES](https://prtimes.jp/main/html/rd/p/000000152.000000888.html)
- **BJT (Business Japanese Proficiency Test, 漢検)**:
  - Format: computer-based since April 2017, with listening, listening-reading and reading sections; no speaking. — [世良 2018](http://global8.or.jp/Ms.Sera_OPIcJ@2018LET.pdf); [Japonin comparison](https://www.japonin.com/japan/japanese-certification-guide.html)
  - Where: Pearson VUE test centers, on any day the center is open. In August 2025 漢検 announced new Vietnamese centers in Hanoi and Da Nang; 2019 information listed Hanoi, Ho Chi Minh City, Da Nang and Hue. — [漢検 BJT notice 2025](https://www.kanken.or.jp/bjt/topics/2025/post_42.html); [BJT English](https://www.kanken.or.jp/bjt/english/); [2019 info](https://visa-zaimu.com/blog/recruit/660.html)
  - VJCC Ho Chi Minh City runs a BJT preparation course. — [VJCC HCM](https://www.vjcchcmc.org.vn/ja/course/coursebjt)
- **J.TEST (実用日本語検定)**: reading and listening only. The listening section has conversation and explanation items, but you never speak. It is held in Vietnam and the official site lists a Vietnam office. A comparison table in the Agency for Cultural Affairs' 2018 survey seems to show no oral component, but the table is hard to read. — [jpsk.jp summary](https://jpsk.jp/examinations/j-test.html); [J.TEST overseas offices](https://j-test.jp/office1); [文化庁 平成30年度日本語教育総合調査](https://www.bunka.go.jp/seisaku/bunkashingikai/kokugo/nihongo/nihongo_93/pdf/r1417436_06.pdf)
- **JFT-Basic**:
  - Format: four sections (文字と語彙, 会話と表現, 聴解, 読解), about 50 questions, 60 minutes; "会話と表現" is multiple choice, not spoken. — [Prometric JFT-Basic](https://www.prometric-jp.com/ssw/test_list/archives/1j)
  - Vietnam: Prometric began delivering it in Ho Chi Minh City on 11 June 2025 and in Hanoi on 24 June 2025. A Japanese Embassy notice lists sessions in Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City from 16 April 2026. — [PR TIMES (Prometric)](https://prtimes.jp/main/html/rd/p/000000009.000147222.html); [Embassy of Japan in Vietnam](https://www.vn.emb-japan.go.jp/itpr_ja/11_000001_02216_Tokuteigino_Shikenjouhou_JFTBasic.html)
  - Levels: from August 2026 results are given as A1, A2.1 or A2.2, and the test can be used for the new 育成就労 work program and for study. — [PR TIMES (JF-related)](https://prtimes.jp/main/html/rd/p/000000061.000079420.html); [JFT-Basic about](https://www.jpf.go.jp/jft-basic/about/index.html)
- **Frameworks**: the Agency for Cultural Affairs' 日本語教育の参照枠 second report (2021) covers how to assess Japanese ability and describes the OPI and OPIc. — [二次報告](https://www.bunka.go.jp/seisaku/bunkashingikai/kokugo/hokoku/pdf/92880801_01.pdf). Its 2018 survey compares the main Japanese tests. — [平成30年度 調査](https://www.bunka.go.jp/seisaku/bunkashingikai/kokugo/nihongo/nihongo_93/pdf/r1417436_06.pdf)

### Inferences
- A practical plan from Vietnam:
  - take the JLPT N3 now and N2 later, the standard written credential;
  - get a speaking benchmark from JSST (cheap and quick, with comments on register, if individual testing is available) or from the remote OPIc. The OPIc's ACTFL levels are more widely recognized internationally;
  - treat mock interviews as the real practice test, since no evidence shows Japanese IT employers require a speaking score.
- BJT is relevant to business Japanese but says nothing about speaking. A good BJT score backs up a candidate's claims but does not replace speaking practice.

### Gaps
- No data on how many Japanese or Vietnamese IT employers recognize OPI, OPIc or JSST scores.
- LTI's current price for individual Japanese OPI/OPIc candidates, and whether booking from Vietnam has any limits, were not confirmed.
- Whether JSST is open to individuals in 2026 conflicts between ALC pages. No JSST-to-CEFR or JSST-to-JLPT mapping turned up.
- Not researched because the search budget ran out: the speaking can-dos in JF日本語教育スタンダード and 日本語教育の参照枠 (the 話すこと（やり取り）/（発表） scales), and any new speaking tests launched in 2025–2026.

## 8. How long from N3 to N2/N1 and to business-level conversation

### Takeaway
No official JLPT or Japan Foundation study-hour survey turned up. The hour figures in circulation come from schools and commercial guides that disagree. They agree that learners with a kanji background need roughly half the hours of non-kanji learners. The FSI puts Japanese in its hardest group at about 2,200 class hours to professional working level. Vietnamese learners fall between the two groups: Hán Việt helps with vocabulary from intermediate level on (Matsuda), but they do not read kanji.

### Cited Findings
- The old (pre-2010) JLPT published guidelines: Level 1 assumed about 2,000 kanji, 10,000 words and 900 study hours. The new N3 had no old equivalent, so it never had published guidelines. — [すらら column](https://surala.jp/column/japanese-ability/18063/)
- One Japanese school's blog cites "1992–2010" data: N1 took 1,800–2,300 hours for students from kanji-using countries and 3,100–4,500 hours for others. I did not identify the primary source. The same set of sites gives rough totals of N5 ≈ 150 h, N4 ≈ 300 h, N3 ≈ 500 h and N2 ≈ 600 h, apparently carried over from the old guidelines. — [ルネサンス日本語学院 blog](https://biz.rn-ac.jp/topics/jlpt0817.html); [newtongym8](https://newtongym8.com/g-nihongons.html)
- A corporate-training blog says kanji-background learners usually need 1–2 years from zero to N1, and non-kanji learners at least twice the hours. — [smarthabit](https://www.smarthabit.net/enterprise/blog/japanease0007)
- Commercial English-language guides disagree. For N2 they give about 1,150–1,800 h with a kanji background vs. 1,600–2,800 h without, or about 1,500 vs. 2,200 h; one claims Japan Foundation/JEES data, which I could not confirm. For N1 they give 3,000–4,800 h or 3,900–4,500 h without a kanji background, and about 1,700–2,100 h with one. At least one table seems to have its kanji and non-kanji labels swapped. — [Coto Academy](https://cotoacademy.com/study-hours-needed-pass-jlpt-comparison-levels/); [thinka.ai](https://www.thinka.ai/en-US/blogs/GJmDXRyaGjS3ehuokSA8/How-Many-Hours-to-Pass-JLPT-N2-Study-Hours-Chart-Kanji-Breakdown-and-December-2026-Timeline)
- FSI: Japanese is in the hardest group, about 88 weeks or 2,200 class hours to reach ILR 3 (professional working proficiency, about ACTFL Advanced High to Superior). It carries an asterisk meaning it is usually harder than the others in that group. Secondary sources call this group Category IV or Category V, and I did not check the current State Department page. — [Atlas & Boots](https://www.atlasandboots.com/foreign-service-institute-language-difficulty/); [CUNY blog](https://chrysanthemum.commons.gc.cuny.edu/2021/05/is-japanese-a-really-difficult-language-the-u-s-department-of-state-thinks-so/)
- OPI rates speaking on 10 levels (Novice to Superior). — [文化審議会 二次報告](https://www.bunka.go.jp/seisaku/bunkashingikai/kokugo/hokoku/pdf/92880801_01.pdf)
- For Vietnamese learners, the Hán Việt match rate is highest in late-intermediate to advanced written vocabulary. — [松田 (researchmap)](https://researchmap.jp/read0103233/published_papers/1795870)

### Inferences
- Rough arithmetic on these unofficial figures: if N3 sits around 500–700 cumulative hours and N2 between about 1,150 and 2,800, then N3 → N2 is roughly 600–2,000 more hours. A Vietnamese learner probably lands in the middle-to-lower part of that range, because Hán Việt helps most with the N2/N1 vocabulary. At 10–15 hours a week alongside a full-time job, that is about 1–3 years. Treat this as a planning range, not a finding.
- JLPT study hours do not translate into speaking ability, since JLPT preparation trains no speaking. For the IT jobs targeted, business-level conversation means handling meetings, reporting and keigo, which on the FSI and ACTFL scales is around Advanced to ILR 3. That needs its own speaking practice (questions 1–3 and 5) on top of JLPT study.

### Gaps
- No official JLPT or Japan Foundation survey of study hours by native language or kanji background was found. No data specific to Vietnamese learners' study hours was found.
- No source was found on whether Japanese education classes Vietnamese learners as "kanji-sphere", "non-kanji-sphere" or in between.
- No source was found that maps "business-level conversation" (the term in job ads) to OPI, JSST or CEFR levels.
- No longitudinal data on how long N3 learners take to reach OPI Intermediate-High or Advanced.
